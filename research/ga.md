## 1. LICENSING & REGULATION — DECAL / Bright from the Start

**Agency.** Bright from the Start: Georgia Department of Early Care and Learning (DECAL) is both the licensing agency and the CCDF Lead Agency — an unusual single-agency consolidation that also runs Pre-K, CACFP, Head Start Collaboration, and the Criminal Records Unit ([decal.ga.gov](https://www.decal.ga.gov/)).

**Governing rules.** Child Care Learning Centers (CCLCs, 7+ children) are governed by **Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. Chapter 591-1-1**. The currently posted consolidated version carries an **effective date of July 1, 2025** ([DECAL CCLC Rules PDF](https://www.decal.ga.gov/documents/attachments/cclcrulesandregulations.pdf)). Family Child Care Learning Homes are Chapter 290-2-3, also republished July 2025 ([ACF Licensing Regulations Database](https://licensingregulations.acf.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/licensing_regulation/GA_FCCH_JULY_2025_508.pdf)).

**2025–2026 rule activity.**
- DECAL held a **public hearing on proposed licensing rule amendments on May 15, 2025** ([DECAL Proposed Rule Revisions](https://www.decal.ga.gov/CCS/ProposedRevisions.aspx)).
- An amendment package **effective June 4, 2025** updated pediatric First Aid and CPR requirements; the consolidated rulebook was reissued **July 1, 2025** ([DECAL CCLC Rules PDF](https://www.decal.ga.gov/documents/attachments/cclcrulesandregulations.pdf)).
- **No new proposed rule package was posted as of early August 2026.** DECAL's Proposed Revisions page currently shows only the May 2025 notice. **UNCONFIRMED** whether a 2026-27 package is in drafting — worth a direct call to DECAL Child Care Services (404-656-5957).

**Ratios and group sizes (Rule 591-1-1-.32; adopted 3/1/1998, last amended 9/20/2020)** ([Cornell LII text of 591-1-1-.32](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/georgia/Ga-Comp-R-Regs-R-591-1-1-.32)):

| Age | Staff:Child | Max group size |
|---|---|---|
| Under 1 yr / under 18 mo non-walking | **1:6** | 12 |
| 1 yr (walking) | **1:8** | 16 |
| 2 yrs | **1:10** | 20 |
| 3 yrs | **1:15** | 30 |
| 4 yrs | **1:18** | 36 |
| 5 yrs | **1:20** | 40 |
| 6+ yrs | **1:25** | 50 |

Group-size caps do **not** apply to outdoor playground time, special center activities of ≤2 hours, or centers licensed for ≤18 children — but ratios must be maintained at all times. **Operator note: Georgia's 3s (1:15) and 4s (1:18) ratios are among the most permissive in the country and are a material margin advantage relative to neighboring states.** Note the separate, tighter Georgia Pre-K standard (§3 below).

**Staff qualifications & training.**
- **Directors:** must complete a **40-hour DECAL-approved director's training course** covering administrator competencies (early learning standards, business management, developmentally appropriate practice) ([CCLC Rules](https://www.decal.ga.gov/documents/attachments/cclcrulesandregulations.pdf)).
- **All direct-care staff:** pediatric **CPR certification on a biennial cycle** and **pediatric first aid on a triennial cycle**, obtained **within the first 45 days of employment** (same source).
- Annual continuing-education hour requirements are set in rule but the exact hour count was not retrievable from the accessible excerpt — **UNCONFIRMED**; verify against 591-1-1-.14/.15 directly. (Georgia has historically required 10 clock hours/yr for staff and 15+ for directors, but do not rely on that without confirming.)

**Background checks — GACCBR (Georgia Child Care Background Records).** ([DECAL Criminal Records Check](https://www.decal.ga.gov/CCS/CriminalRecordsCheck.aspx))
- Fingerprint-based checks required for **every person working at a CCLC, FCCLH, or license-exempt program receiving CAPS funds**.
- Two determinations: **National Records Check** (permits *supervised* work while out-of-state records pend) and **Comprehensive Records Check** (required for *unsupervised* access). Directors and employees need a satisfactory Comprehensive determination letter issued **within the preceding 12 months of hire**.
- **Renewal every 5 years**, or after the first year if the employee changes facilities.
- **Portability:** determination letters less than one year old can be electronically ported to a different facility without a new check — operationally significant for multi-site operators moving staff between centers.
- **DECAL pays the fingerprinting cost** for child care employees "for the foreseeable future unless funding becomes no longer available." **Watch item: this is an explicitly conditional state subsidy of a compliance cost.**

**Inspection & enforcement.** DECAL posts enforcement actions publicly and uses a graduated ladder (letters of concern → corrective action plans → administrative fines → license suspension/revocation). **The DECAL Enforcement Actions page was inaccessible during this research (robots/SSL block), so 2025–2026 enforcement volumes and trend direction are UNCONFIRMED.** This is the single largest data gap in the licensing section and should be filled by (a) an open-records request to DECAL for FY2024–FY2026 enforcement action counts by type, and (b) pulling your own centers' inspection histories from the DECAL search portal.

**New family-facing system.** DECAL launched **DECAL FAST (Family Access to Services & Tools)** on **July 10, 2026** at families.decal.ga.gov — a consolidated family portal with an AI navigation chatbot ("CALi"), Spanish-language content, and mobile-first design ([WLAQ, 7/10/2026](https://www.wlaq1410.com/2026/07/10/ga-decal-introduces-decal-fast-family-access-to-services-tools/)). Provider-side implications were not detailed. **Action item: confirm whether FAST changes how your centers appear in state-facing search/referral, and whether Quality Rated status is surfaced more prominently to families.**

## 2. SUBSIDY / CCDF — Childcare and Parent Services (CAPS)

**The headline for this client: Georgia has materially *contracted* its subsidy program since 2024, and the federal floor beneath it was removed in July 2026.**

**Funding.**
- **FY2026: ~$77 million** state funds for CAPS ([GBPI FY2026 Education Primer](https://gbpi.org/georgia-education-primer-for-state-fiscal-year-2026/)).
- **FY2027 (enacted): $77.5 million** state funds ([GBPI FY2027 Education Primer, 6/30/2026](https://gbpi.org/georgia-education-primer-for-state-fiscal-year-2027/)).
- CAPS funding grew only **25% over eight years against 31% inflation** — a real-terms cut — while Pre-K grew 57% (same source).
- Georgia lost **$170M+ in federal COVID-era relief in September 2024** ([GBPI FY2027 DECAL Overview, 2/11/2026](https://gbpi.org/overview-2027-fiscal-year-budget-for-the-georgia-department-of-early-care-and-learning/)); GEEARS puts the resulting gap at **~$188 million** ([GEEARS CAPS one-pager](https://geears.org/wp-content/uploads/Child-Care-One-Pager.pdf)).

**Eligibility — the critical change.** DECAL **cut initial income eligibility from 50% of State Median Income ($52,947 for a family of four) to 30% of SMI ($31,768 for a family of four)** — described by GBPI as **the lowest initial eligibility threshold in the country** ([GBPI FY2026 Education Primer](https://gbpi.org/georgia-education-primer-for-state-fiscal-year-2026/)). Families must also participate in **≥24 hours/week of approved work or school activity** and fall within a DECAL-designated **priority group** ([GEEARS](https://geears.org/wp-content/uploads/Child-Care-One-Pager.pdf)).
- Continued (redetermination) eligibility runs to the federal ceiling of **85% SMI** — reported at roughly $94,126 for a family of four ([DaycareCalc GA, 2026](https://daycarecalc.com/subsidy-eligibility/georgia)). *Secondary source; confirm against CAPS Appendix A.*
- **CAPS Appendix A (Maximum Income Limits by Family Size) carries an effective date of March 2, 2026** ([caps.decal.ga.gov Appendix A](https://caps.decal.ga.gov/assets/downloads/CAPS/AppendixA-CAPS%20Maximum%20Income%20Limits%20by%20Family%20Size.pdf) — direct fetch blocked; URL and effective date confirmed via search index). **Recommend pulling this PDF manually.**
- **Note a live conflict in the record:** GEEARS' CAPS one-pager still states 50% SMI. Treat **30% SMI as the operative initial threshold** (GBPI is the more recent and more specific source), but verify with DECAL.

**Enrollment.**
- Peak of roughly **72,000 children (2023)**, now roughly **51,000** — GEEARS' 2026 agenda explicitly seeks to "restore CAPS enrollment to 2023 levels" ([GEEARS Policy](https://geears.org/policy/)).
- **~24,000 children lost scholarships** when pandemic funding ended in 2024; the expired federal funds had supported **22,000+ scholarships** ([GEEARS](https://geears.org/wp-content/uploads/Child-Care-One-Pager.pdf); [GBPI FY2026](https://gbpi.org/georgia-education-primer-for-state-fiscal-year-2026/)).
- Only about **7% of income-eligible children** are served ([GEEARS Policy](https://geears.org/policy/)); GBPI cites ~18% of *eligible families* reached in 2020 ([GBPI FY2027 DECAL Overview](https://gbpi.org/overview-2027-fiscal-year-budget-for-the-georgia-department-of-early-care-and-learning/)).
- **Recent adds are small:** FY2026 added **$4M for 500 additional scholarships**; FY2027 redirected **$9.7M in freed federal funds to serve 1,288 more children** ([GBPI FY2026](https://gbpi.org/georgia-education-primer-for-state-fiscal-year-2026/); [GBPI Crossover Day 2026](https://gbpi.org/crossover-day-2026-update-house-passes-fiscal-year-2027-budget/)).
- **Formal waitlist status: UNCONFIRMED.** Georgia manages access through the eligibility threshold and priority groups rather than a published queue; DECAL does not appear to post a waitlist count. Recommend an open-records request for pending/denied application volumes.

**Reimbursement rate methodology.**
- Rates are set at the **60th percentile of the most recent market rate survey** ([GEEARS](https://geears.org/wp-content/uploads/Child-Care-One-Pager.pdf)). FY2026 included **$1.5M to annualize the state match** for the 60th-percentile rate ([GBPI FY2026 DECAL Overview](https://gbpi.org/overview-2026-fiscal-year-budget-for-the-georgia-department-of-early-care-and-learning/)).
- The current rate basis is a **2024 analysis by Care Solutions, Inc.** of 2023 state administrative rate data — *not* a fresh provider survey. It includes appendices on statewide statistics, expanded rate data, and **Quality Rated rates by zone** ([DECAL Market Rate Studies](https://www.decal.ga.gov/Research/ResearchMarketRates.aspx)).
- **A new market study is in progress in 2026**, conducted with **Georgia Policy Labs at Georgia State University**, in two parts: a **Child Care Market Rate Survey** (all licensed providers) and a **Child Care Cost Survey** (selected Quality Rated CAPS providers) — the latter is the "alternative methodology"/cost-estimation approach the 2024 federal rule encouraged (same source; contact ChildCareMarketStudy@decal.ga.gov). **This is the single most consequential pending item for CAPS rate levels. Your operator should participate in the cost survey — cost-based methodologies generally produce higher rate benchmarks than market-price surveys.**
- Rates vary by **zone, age group, and Quality Rated star level** (tiered reimbursement). Specific 2026 dollar rate tables were not retrievable (caps.decal.ga.gov is robots-blocked) — **UNCONFIRMED at the line-item level**; pull the current CAPS rate chart directly from the provider portal.

**Family copay.** Sliding scale keyed to Federal Poverty Level bands, one fee per family divided across children: **no fee at ≤10% FPL; 3% of annual income at 10–50% FPL; 5% at 50–100% FPL; 7% above 100% FPL** ([DaycareCalc GA](https://daycarecalc.com/subsidy-eligibility/georgia)). *Secondary source — verify against CAPS Policy Manual §00-9.*

**The 2024 federal CCDF rule — and its 2026 reversal.** This is the most important recent federal development.
- The **March 2024 CCDF final rule** required states to cap family copays at **7% of income**, pay providers **prospectively**, pay **based on enrollment rather than attendance**, and use **grants/contracts** for underserved populations ([ACF overview](https://acf.gov/occ/law-regulation/overview-2024-ccdf-final-rule-improving-child-care-access-affordability)).
- **On May 12, 2026, HHS/ACF published "Restoring Flexibility in the Child Care and Development Fund" (91 FR 25796; Doc. 2026-09382; RIN 0970-AD20), effective July 13, 2026** ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/12/2026-09382/restoring-flexibility-in-the-child-care-and-development-fund-ccdf); [ACF](https://acf.gov/occ/law-regulation/2026-ccdf-final-rule)). It **rescinds all four** requirements:
  - §98.45(l)(3) — the **7% copay cap** is gone; states need only show fees are on a sliding scale and not a "barrier."
  - §98.30(b)(1) — the **grants/contracts mandate** is gone.
  - §98.45(m)(1) — **prospective payment** replaced with a choice of prospective *or* payment **within 21 days of invoice**.
  - §98.45(m)(2) — **enrollment-based payment** repealed; states may return to **attendance-based** or an **85% attendance threshold** model.
- Nationally, **nearly all states had capped copays at ≤7% and 25 states + DC had adopted enrollment-based payment**, so most are expected to hold in place voluntarily ([NAEYC](https://www.naeyc.org/our-work/public-policy-advocacy/2026-ccdf-final-rule); [Southern Education Foundation, 6/4/2026](https://southerneducation.org/resources/blog/2026/06/04/hhs-finalizes-changes-to-child-care-subsidy-rules-what-it-means-for-families-providers-and-states/)).
- **Georgia's payment basis: at least one industry source states Georgia operates an attendance-based payment model with mandatory date-stamped digital attendance documentation for CAPS reimbursement** ([brightwheel GA licensing, verified April 2026](https://mybrightwheel.com/licensing-requirements/georgia)). This is a secondary source and **should be treated as PLAUSIBLE, NOT CONFIRMED** — it is the highest-value open question in this profile for your revenue model. Get the answer from DECAL's CAPS Policy Manual §00-12 or your CAPS provider agreement.
- **Practical read:** because the federal floor is gone as of July 13, 2026, whatever Georgia's current practice is, there is now **no federal barrier to Georgia reverting to or retaining attendance-based, in-arrears payment** — a direct cash-flow and bad-debt exposure for centers with high CAPS census.

**CCDF plan & policy manual cadence.** Georgia's **FFY2025–2027 CCDF State Plan** is posted at [decal.ga.gov](https://www.decal.ga.gov/documents/attachments/CCDFStatePlan25-27.pdf) (direct fetch blocked). CAPS Policy Manual sections were revised **07/01/2025** (Eligibility Requirements), **10/06/2025** (Definitions; Application Process — added a verification-document grace period and clarified authorized representatives), and **03/02/2026** (Parent Rights & Responsibilities — added SMS opt-in and clarified cooperation with eligibility reviews) ([CAPS Policy Manual](https://www.decal.ga.gov/caps/CAPSPolicyManualOne.aspx)).

## 3. GEORGIA PRE-K — Lottery-Funded

**This is the most attractive and most stable revenue line in Georgia for a private operator, and it has been getting steadily better funded.**

**Funding levels.**
| | FY2026 | FY2027 (enacted) |
|---|---|---|
| DECAL total state funds | ~$640M (4% over FY2025's $621M) | **$671.5M** (+$31.1M, +5%) |
| Georgia Pre-K | **$563M** | **$594M** |
| CAPS | ~$77M | **$77.5M** |

Sources: [GBPI FY2026 Education Primer](https://gbpi.org/georgia-education-primer-for-state-fiscal-year-2026/); [GBPI FY2026 DECAL Overview, 2/12/2025](https://gbpi.org/overview-2026-fiscal-year-budget-for-the-georgia-department-of-early-care-and-learning/); [GBPI FY2027 Education Primer, 6/30/2026](https://gbpi.org/georgia-education-primer-for-state-fiscal-year-2027/).

**FY2027 Pre-K additions — $30.7–30.8M total** ([GBPI FY2027 Primer](https://gbpi.org/georgia-education-primer-for-state-fiscal-year-2027/); [GBPI Crossover Day 2026, 3/13/2026](https://gbpi.org/crossover-day-2026-update-house-passes-fiscal-year-2027-budget/)):
- **$12M** — year **three** of the four-year phase-in reducing class size from 22 to 20
- **$9.7M** — extended-day expansion for priority groups (children experiencing homelessness, children in foster care); lottery-funded before/after-school scholarships
- **$4M** — **five days of paid leave** for Pre-K lead and assistant teachers (new)
- **$5.9M** — employer health-benefit plan cost increases for Pre-K teachers (in Governor's recommendation)
- **$708K** — Rising Pre-K Summer Transition Program expansion

**Class size and ratio — the 22→20 reduction is effectively complete in program rules.** DECAL's **2026-2027 Pre-K Application Overview** states **"the maximum class size for all Georgia's Pre-K classrooms is 20 students,"** with **inclusion classrooms capped at 18**, staffed by **one Lead Teacher plus one Assistant Teacher** ([DECAL Pre-K Application Overview](https://www.decal.ga.gov/documents/attachments/Pre-KApplicationOverview.pdf)). NIEER records the standard as **max 20 with a 1:10 ratio** for 2024-2025 ([NIEER Georgia 2024-2025 profile](https://nieer.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/georgia_2025.pdf)) — up from **max 22 / 1:11** in 2023-2024 ([NIEER Georgia 2023-2024](https://nieer.org/sites/default/files/2025-04/georgia_2024.pdf)). **The budget appropriation phase-in (year 3 of 4 in FY2027) is funding the revenue backfill for classrooms that lost two slots, not a further rule change.**

**Program requirements (2026-2027).** 180 instructional days + 10 professional development/planning days; **6.5-hour instructional day**; DECAL-approved curriculum; new classrooms receive a **$30,000 startup grant** for materials and equipment. Private centers qualify if **DECAL-licensed**, with licensed space for at least 20 children and a playground accommodating 20. **Application window: February 23 – March 18, 2026 (5:00 p.m.); incomplete applications are not reviewed.** The FY2026-2027 rate chart is published only **after gubernatorial budget approval** ([DECAL Pre-K Application Overview](https://www.decal.ga.gov/documents/attachments/Pre-KApplicationOverview.pdf)).

**Enrollment and per-child spending** ([NIEER](https://nieer.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/georgia_2025.pdf)):
- **2024-2025: 70,572 children enrolled = 53% of Georgia 4-year-olds**; **$538.4M** total state spending; **$7,630 per child** (a 22% budget increase YoY, including ~$46M for teacher salaries).
- **2023-2024: 71,526 enrolled; $457.8M; $6,400 per child.**
- Georgia met **10 of 10 NIEER quality benchmarks in 2024-2025**, up from **8 of 10 in 2023-2024** — driven by the class-size/ratio reduction. **This is a useful credential in state-level advocacy.**

**Teacher compensation.** Lead teacher salaries are tied to the **State Board of Education K-12 salary schedule** (pay parity), funded with **$7.4M in the amended FY2025 budget** ([GBPI FY2026 DECAL Overview](https://gbpi.org/overview-2026-fiscal-year-budget-for-the-georgia-department-of-early-care-and-learning/)). 2024-2025 also delivered a **27.5% assistant teacher salary increase** and funding for **100 new classrooms** ([NIEER](https://nieer.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/georgia_2025.pdf)). FY2026 included **$18.8M for one-time $2,000 raises, of which $17.2M went to Pre-K teachers** ([GBPI FY2027 DECAL Overview](https://gbpi.org/overview-2027-fiscal-year-budget-for-the-georgia-department-of-early-care-and-learning/)). Pre-K funding scales with teacher credential level — GBPI's historical example cites **$38,821 allocated per lead teacher with a four-year degree and teaching certificate** ([GBPI Betting on our Future](https://gbpi.org/betting-on-our-future-results-from-pre-k-survey-on-lottery-funding-effects-of-covid-19/)). **Operator implication: Georgia Pre-K salary parity is a wage-floor pull on your non-Pre-K infant/toddler staff. It is the primary reason Pre-K classrooms and infant rooms in the same building have divergent labor economics.**

**Private provider share.** DECAL states participation is "voluntary for public schools and for private child development centers," delivered through "local public schools or through private providers" ([DECAL Pre-K About](https://www.decal.ga.gov/Prek/About.aspx)). GBPI's survey research found **enrollment was roughly evenly split between public schools and private providers across FY2012–FY2019** ([GBPI](https://gbpi.org/betting-on-our-future-results-from-pre-k-survey-on-lottery-funding-effects-of-covid-19/)). NIEER confirms delivery through "public schools, private child-care centers, faith-based organizations, Head Start agencies, state colleges and universities, and military facilities" ([NIEER](https://nieer.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/georgia_2025.pdf)). **A current (2025-26) precise public/private class count is UNCONFIRMED** — DECAL's provider-resources pages were inaccessible. Use "approximately half" and request the current split from DECAL's Pre-K division. **The ~50% private share is the core political fact for your government-affairs posture in Georgia: no other state pre-K program of this scale is so dependent on for-profit and community providers, which gives your sector real standing at the table.**

**Expansion pressure.** GEEARS is pushing a **pilot to serve three-year-olds** and points to **$1.7 billion in unrestricted Lottery reserves** as the funding source ([GEEARS Policy](https://geears.org/policy/)). The vehicle in 2026 was **HB 1146** (see §5) — it died, but the reserve balance makes this a recurring ask.

## 4. QUALITY RATED — Georgia's QRIS

- **Structure:** voluntary **1-, 2-, or 3-star** system for centers, family homes, and school-age programs. 1 star = exceeds minimum licensing; 3 stars = highest quality ([DECAL Quality Rated](https://www.decal.ga.gov/qualityinitiatives/qualityrated.aspx)).
- **Scoring (Program Manual v2.7.5):** 0–14 pts = no stars; 15–24 = 1 star; 25–35 = 2 stars; 36–45 = 3 stars. Points come from **Structural Quality (portfolio, 0–15)**, **Process Quality (ERS classroom observation, 0–30)**, and an **accreditation bonus (up to 4)**. Five portfolio standards: director/teacher qualifications; child health, nutrition & physical activity; family engagement; intentional teaching practices; teacher-student ratios. **Ratings are valid three years**, then require reassessment ([Quality Rated Program Manual](https://qualityrated.decal.ga.gov/Content/Documents/PM_ProgramManual.pdf)). *Note: the posted manual version is dated June 1, 2019 — DECAL directs users to the online version as authoritative.*
- **Tie to subsidy — this is mandatory, not optional:** **"As of December 31, 2021, all child care providers must be Quality Rated (or in Provisional or Probationary Status) to receive CAPS funding"** ([DECAL Quality Rated](https://www.decal.ga.gov/qualityinitiatives/qualityrated.aspx)). Quality Rated also drives **tiered CAPS reimbursement** and **Quality Rated rates by zone** appear as a distinct appendix in the market rate analysis ([DECAL Market Rate Studies](https://www.decal.ga.gov/Research/ResearchMarketRates.aspx)).
- **Financial incentives:** Annual **Quality Rated Workforce Bonus**, **Commitment to Quality (C2Q) Payments**, customizable bonus packages covering credentialing, equipment, curriculum and PD, plus free technical assistance via regional CCR&R agencies. Payments are administered through [decalqrpayments.com](https://decalqrpayments.com/). Current dollar amounts **UNCONFIRMED**.
- **Recent changes:** DECAL ran a **"Quality Rated Updates for 2026"** webinar and a **Georgia Child Care Quality Study (2025)**; a **proposed Quality Rated Framework revision was discussed in May 2024** ([DECAL Quality Rated](https://www.decal.ga.gov/qualityinitiatives/qualityrated.aspx)). **The status of that framework revision — whether it has been adopted, and what it changes about point thresholds or ERS weighting — is UNCONFIRMED and is a priority follow-up**, because a re-weighted framework can move star levels and therefore tiered reimbursement across an entire portfolio at once.
- **Current participation counts: UNCONFIRMED.** The only participation data retrievable is stale (July 2014: ~380 of 5,454 providers rated, ~7%; distribution 24% 1-star / 44% 2-star / 31% 3-star) ([GOEWS/GOSA](https://goews.georgia.gov/introduction-quality-rated-georgias-tiered-quality-rating-and-improvement-system-child-care)). Given the 2021 CAPS mandate, current participation is certainly far higher. Request current counts from DECAL.

## 5. 2025–2026 LEGISLATION & BUDGETS

**Session mechanics.** The 2025–2026 biennium ran two sessions. **2026 session: Crossover Day March 6, 2026; Sine Die April 2, 2026**; passed and signed legislation took effect **July 1, 2026** unless otherwise specified ([GEEARS Crossover Update, 3/16/2026](https://geears.org/news/georgias-2026-legislative-session-an-update-at-crossover-day/); [PAGE 2026 Session Summary](https://www.pagelegislative.org/post/2026-legislative-session-summary); [Dentons Capitol Insights, GA Edition, April 2026](https://www.dentons.com/en/insights/newsletters/2026/april/27/dentons-capitol-insights-georgia-edition/-/media/A262250CF2304879A38F7D56557C3B0F.ashx)).

### HB 136 (2025) — the child care tax credit package. **PASSED. Act 182.**
Lead sponsor **Rep. Mark Newton (R-Augusta)**, with Reps. Shaw Blackmon, Trey Rhodes, Brent Cox, Brian Strickland (R) and Brian Prince, Sheila Jones (D) ([LegiScan HB136](https://legiscan.com/GA/bill/HB136/2025)). Carried as a **priority of Lt. Gov. Burt Jones** and derived from the recommendations of the **Senate Study Committee on Access to Affordable Childcare** ([Lt. Gov. press release, 5/13/2025](https://ltgov.georgia.gov/press-releases/2025-05-13/lt-governor-jones-priority-signed-law-child-tax-credit-and-childcare-tax)).

**Path:** House 170–2 (Feb 24, 2025) → Senate 50–0 (Mar 28, 2025) → **signed May 13, 2025** → **Act 182, effective July 1, 2025** ([LegiScan](https://legiscan.com/GA/bill/HB136/2025)).

**Provisions** ([signed bill text](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2025-signed-legislation/hb-136/download)):

| Credit | Terms | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **Child & Dependent Care Expense credit (CDCTC)** | Raised from **30% → 50% of the federal credit**, flat | **Tax years beginning on/after Jan 1, 2025** |
| **Georgia Child Tax Credit (new)** | **$250 per qualifying child under age 6** | **Tax years beginning on/after Jan 1, 2026** |
| **Employer Child Care Credit (new)** | **$1,000 per child in the first year** of participation, **$500/child annually** thereafter; requires **direct employer payments of ≥$1,000/yr per employee** to a **licensed** facility for employees' children **under age 6**; **$20 million annual aggregate statewide cap** | **Effective Jan 1, 2026**; **sunsets Dec 31, 2030** |
| Foster Care Support Org credit (expansion) | Aggregate cap $20M (2025), **$30M (2026+)**, $10M max for insurers; 3-yr carryforward | 2025 forward |

All three child care credits are **nonrefundable, capped at the taxpayer's income tax liability, with no carryforward**. **This is a meaningful limitation: employers with low Georgia income tax liability capture little value, and the $20M statewide cap on the employer credit means first-come allocation matters.**

**Georgia's pre-existing employer child care credit (O.C.G.A. §48-7-40.6) remains in force and is far larger in value than the new HB 136 credit** ([Justia, §48-7-40.6](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-48/chapter-7/article-2/section-48-7-40-6/)):
- **Operating cost credit: 75% of the employer's cost of operation** less employee payments; capped at **50% of annual income tax liability**; **5-year carryforward**.
- **Qualified child care property credit: 100% of cost, claimed at 10%/year over 10 years**; capped at **50% of liability**; **3-year carryforward**; the facility must be **DECAL-licensed** and **≥95% of users must be children of participating employees**; recapture applies on a declining scale (100% within 5 years → 10% in year 14 → 0% thereafter).
- Amended by **2024 Ga. Laws 701, effective July 1, 2024**.
- **This is the credit to build employer-partnership offerings around — the on-site/near-site model.** The 95% test is the binding constraint for a shared-employer or consortium center.

## 8. BUSINESS-CLIMATE ISSUES FOR A FOR-PROFIT OPERATOR

**Workforce and wages — the binding constraint.**
- Georgia child care worker **median hourly wage $13.35 / annual median $27,760 (2023)** ([GBPI, From Barriers to Bridges](https://gbpi.org/from-barriers-to-bridges-expanding-access-to-child-care-and-improving-upward-mobility-for-georgias-child-care-workers/)).
- Infant-toddler teachers earn **less than Pre-K teachers**, who benefit from lottery-funded pay schedules — **the state has effectively created a two-tier wage structure inside your own buildings.** Black women teaching infants and toddlers earn about **$0.77/hour less** than other teachers of the same age group (same source).
- Workforce headcount declined for most of the 2010s but **improved notably between 2022 and 2023**, tracking wage increases (same source).
- **Georgia Pre-K lead teacher salary parity with K-12** plus the **27.5% assistant teacher raise (2024-25)** and **five days of new paid leave (FY2027)** are all wage-floor pressure that transmits to non-Pre-K rooms ([NIEER](https://nieer.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/georgia_2025.pdf); [GBPI FY2027 Primer](https://gbpi.org/georgia-education-primer-for-state-fiscal-year-2027/)).
- Offsets available: **Quality Rated Workforce Bonus** and **C2Q payments** ([decalqrpayments.com](https://decalqrpayments.com/)); **DECAL pays employee fingerprinting costs** ([DECAL](https://www.decal.ga.gov/CCS/CriminalRecordsCheck.aspx)); GEEARS is pushing to **prioritize early educators' own children for CAPS scholarships**, which would function as a retention subsidy ([GEEARS](https://geears.org/policy/)).

**Market structure / demand.**
- **~4,500 licensed providers; ~368,000 licensed slots (ages 0-12) against ~1,115,000 children under 13 with all available parents working** — a roughly 3:1 gap (2020 data) ([GBPI](https://gbpi.org/from-barriers-to-bridges-expanding-access-to-child-care-and-improving-upward-mobility-for-georgias-child-care-workers/)).
- **44% of Georgians live in a child care desert** (same source) — meaningful for site selection and for the political case for capital grants.
- Georgia's child care shortfall costs the state economy at least **$1.75 billion annually** (2018 analysis, same source) — the standard business-case statistic in Georgia advocacy.

**Liability and insurance — active risk.** **HB 1281 (2026)** would have required CCLCs to carry statutorily specified minimum liability coverage and file proof with DECAL. It died in House Education on **Feb 17, 2026**, but had **six Republican sponsors** ([LegiScan HB1281](https://legiscan.com/GA/bill/HB1281/2025)). In a hardening child care liability market this is a live 2027 refile risk. **Assess: (a) do your current Georgia limits meet plausible statutory minimums, and (b) would a state mandate advantage you against undercapitalized independents? A well-calibrated mandate is often net-favorable to a large operator — consider engaging constructively on limits rather than opposing.**

**Employer child care tax credits — the commercial opportunity.** Two stacked programs:
1. **O.C.G.A. §48-7-40.6** — **75% of operating cost** and **100% of qualified child care property** (10%/yr over 10 yrs), each capped at 50% of Georgia income tax liability, with 5- and 3-year carryforwards, a DECAL licensing requirement, a **95% employee-children test**, and a 14-year declining recapture schedule ([Justia §48-7-40.6](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-48/chapter-7/article-2/section-48-7-40-6/)).
2. **HB 136's new employer credit** — **$1,000 first year / $500 per child annually**, triggered by employer payments of **≥$1,000/yr per employee** to a **licensed** facility for a child **under 6**; **$20M statewide annual cap**; **effective Jan 1, 2026; sunsets Dec 31, 2030** ([HB 136 as passed](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2025-signed-legislation/hb-136/download)).
**Go-to-market read: §48-7-40.6 is the on-site/near-site consortium play; HB 136 is the tuition-benefit play. The HB 136 credit is small and nonrefundable, but its low threshold ($1,000/yr per employee) makes it a workable door-opener with mid-size Georgia employers — and the "licensed facility" requirement means every licensed center qualifies as the recipient. Build a Georgia employer-partnership offering marketed explicitly around both credits, and note the 2030 sunset as urgency.**

**Family-side demand subsidies.** The **CDCTC at 50% of the federal credit** (TY2025+) and the **$250 per-child-under-6 credit** (TY2026+) modestly increase household ability to pay ([HB 136](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2025-signed-legislation/hb-136/download)) — useful in tuition communications for the 2026 tax year.

**Property tax.** Not researched in depth here. Note that **gubernatorial nominee Rick Jackson campaigns on freezing property taxes** ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Rick_Jackson_(Georgia))). Georgia's local property tax treatment of child care facilities, and any exemption for facilities serving CAPS children, is **UNCONFIRMED — flagged as a research gap.**

**Zoning and land use.** **No state-level preemption, by-right siting provision, or zoning reform for child care facilities was identified in the 2025 or 2026 sessions — UNCONFIRMED.** Georgia child care siting appears to be entirely local. **This is an unaddressed policy space and a plausible 2027 ask** (several states have passed by-right child care zoning; SB 554's capital-grant framing is a natural vehicle to attach it to).
