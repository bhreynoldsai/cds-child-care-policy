# ARIZONA — CHILD CARE POLICY, REGULATION & FUNDING PROFILE
**Prepared for:** Multi-state for-profit operator (~30 Phoenix-metro preschools, "Sunrise Preschools")
**Research date:** August 3, 2026 | **Sources:** primary state/federal documents where available; all claims linked inline

---

## EXECUTIVE ORIENTATION

Arizona is a **low-regulation, low-subsidy, divided-government** state with one unusual asset (First Things First, a voter-created tobacco-tax early childhood agency) that is structurally decaying, and one unusual liability for subsidy-dependent providers (a subsidy waitlist that has grown **575% in 24 months**). For a Maricopa County for-profit operator, the three dominant near-term variables are: (1) the DES subsidy waitlist choking Title-supported enrollment, (2) the August 2026 compliance deadline on new ADHS staff training rules, and (3) CPI-indexed minimum wage compression on margins.

## 1. LICENSING & REGULATION — ADHS Bureau of Child Care Licensing

### Governing authority
Child care centers are licensed by the **Arizona Department of Health Services (ADHS), Bureau of Child Care Licensing**, under **A.A.C. Title 9, Chapter 5** and A.R.S. Title 36, Ch. 7.1. ([ADHS Child Care Facilities Licensing](https://www.azdhs.gov/licensing/childcare-facilities/index.php))

### Current rule version — CONFIRMED
The operative Administrative Code chapter is **A.A.C. Title 9, Ch. 5, Supplement 25-2, effective August 3, 2025**. ([AZ Secretary of State, Title 9 Ch. 5 PDF](https://apps.azsos.gov/public_services/Title_09/9-05.pdf))

**Note a document trap:** the PDF ADHS still serves at `azdhs.gov/documents/licensing/childcare-facilities/rules/bccl-child-care-facility-rules.pdf` is the **superseded September 30, 2010** version and still uses "teacher-caregiver." ([stale ADHS rules PDF](https://www.azdhs.gov/documents/licensing/childcare-facilities/rules/bccl-child-care-facility-rules.pdf)) Cornell's mirror of R9-5-404 is likewise keyed to the 2010 amendment. ([Cornell, Ariz. Admin. Code § R9-5-404](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/arizona/Ariz-Admin-Code-SS-R9-5-404)) **Compliance staff should work from the SOS Supp. 25-2 text, not the ADHS-hosted PDF.**

### 2025 rule revision — what changed (effective Aug 3, 2025)
Per First Things First's Quality First summary of the ADHS rulemaking ([Quality First: ADHS rules update](https://www.qualityfirstaz.com/adhs-rules-update/)):

| Area | Change |
|---|---|
| **Job titles** | "Teacher-Caregiver" → **"Child Educator"**; "Assistant Teacher-Caregiver" → **"Assistant Child Educator"**; also "child educator aide" (confirmed in Supp. 25-2 text) |
| **Annual training** | Increased to **24 hours/year** — **compliance deadline August 2026** (from 18 hours under the 2010 rule) |
| **New-staff orientation** | Expanded to cover infant tummy time, SIDS prevention, shaken baby syndrome |
| **Closure reporting** | Must notify ADHS **within 24 hours** of any closure, planned or unplanned; calendars may be shared with compliance officers for expected closures |
| **TB screening** | Mandatory Mantoux testing replaced by a **self-screening form** (programs may still require testing) |
| **Infant sleep** | Cribs: **fitted sheets only**; blankets prohibited |
| **Discipline** | Section reframed as **"Positive Discipline and Guidance"** |
| **Suspension/expulsion** | Programs must adopt **written suspension and expulsion policies** with prevention strategies |
| **Child records** | Customized forms permitted in lieu of state templates (**max two pages**) |
| **Attendance** | "Signature or other unique identifier" accepted — fingerprints, passcodes (enables digital check-in systems) |
| **Plans** | Site/floor plans **no longer require an architect's seal**; architect, draftsman, or contractor acceptable |

**Group home rules remain under review** as of the Quality First summary — status as of August 2026 is **UNCONFIRMED**. Low relevance for a center-based operator.

### Ratios and group size
Ratios appear in **R9-5-404**. The best-available text (2010 compilation, and the section was **not** flagged as amended in the 2025 rulemaking summaries):

| Age | Minimum staff:child ratio |
|---|---|
| Infants (under 1) | **1:5** or 2:11 |
| 1-year-olds | **1:6** or 2:13 |
| 2-year-olds | **1:8** |
| 3-year-olds | **1:13** |
| 4-year-olds | **1:15** |
| 5-year-olds (not school-age) | **1:20** |
| School-age | **1:20** |

([Cornell R9-5-404](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/arizona/Ariz-Admin-Code-SS-R9-5-404); [stale ADHS PDF, same table](https://www.azdhs.gov/documents/licensing/childcare-facilities/rules/bccl-child-care-facility-rules.pdf))

**Group size:** Arizona does **not** impose hard maximum group sizes in R9-5-404. Instead the rule requires the licensee to "determine and maintain the required staff-to-children ratio for each group of enrolled children **based on the age of the youngest child in the group**." That mixed-age rule is the operationally binding constraint, not a group-size cap.

> **UNCONFIRMED / VERIFY:** I could not retrieve the R9-5-404 text out of the Supp. 25-2 PDF (the fetch truncated before p. 21). Ratios are **very likely unchanged** — the 2025 rulemaking summaries describe no ratio change, and secondary code mirrors still date R9-5-404 to 2010 — but **do not treat "ratios unchanged" as fully verified.** Pull pp. 18–22 of the [SOS PDF](https://apps.azsos.gov/public_services/Title_09/9-05.pdf) directly to confirm.

**Competitive read:** Arizona's ratios are among the **most permissive in the country** (1:13 at age 3 and 1:15 at age 4 are national outliers). This is a margin advantage for a for-profit operator and a standing target for advocacy groups. Any future ratio tightening would be a material cost event.

### Staff qualifications (R9-5-401)
Under the 2010 baseline (titles now renamed; substantive thresholds believed carried forward — **verify against Supp. 25-2**):

- **Facility Director** — 21+; one of: 24 months child care experience + HS diploma + 6 college credits in ECE/child development; **or** 18 months experience + CDA/NAC/CCP credential; **or** AA in related field + 6 months experience; **or** BA in related field + 3 months experience.
- **Director's Designee** — 21+, 12 months experience, comparable (slightly reduced) education.
- **Child Educator** (formerly Teacher-Caregiver) — **18+**, 6 months experience + HS diploma, or relevant degree/credential.
- **Assistant Child Educator** — **16+**; current HS enrollment, diploma, or 12 months prior relevant experience.

The 16-year-old floor for assistants and 18/HS-diploma floor for lead educators are permissive relative to peer states — relevant to your staffing pipeline strategy.

**Minimum staffing floor:** director/designee **plus one additional staff member** whenever 6+ children are present; director/designee alone with 5 or fewer, with a second staffer reachable by phone and able to arrive **within 15 minutes**.

### Training (R9-5-403)
- **24 hours annually** per staff member providing child care services (2025 rule; up from 18) — **August 2026 compliance deadline**.
- New staff: orientation within **10 days** covering 17 enumerated topics.
- Infant-care staff: **6+ hours/year** in infant development.
- Directors: **6+ hours/year** in program administration.

### Background checks — DPS Fingerprint Clearance Cards
**R9-5-203(A)–(C):** every staff member must submit a **valid Arizona DPS Fingerprint Clearance Card before the staff member's starting date**, with renewed copies filed at each reissuance. Cards are issued by the **Arizona Department of Public Safety**, valid **6 years**, and are portable across employers — a genuine hiring-velocity advantage in Arizona versus states with employer-specific checks, but the "before start date" rule means **no conditional/provisional start** while a card is pending. Budget for card lead time in your onboarding funnel.

### Inspection & enforcement
- **Pre-licensure inspection** plus **annual unannounced inspections** by ADHS. ([brightwheel, Maricopa local permits](https://mybrightwheel.com/licensing-requirements/arizona/maricopa/local-permits-for-childcare-centers-in-maricopa-county/))
- **UNCONFIRMED:** I could not obtain 2025–2026 ADHS enforcement statistics (citation counts, revocation/suspension trends, top-cited rules). My web-search budget was exhausted before reaching them. **Recommend a direct public-records request to ADHS BCCL** for FY2025–FY2026 enforcement actions by facility and top-10 cited rule sections — this is the single most useful missing dataset for benchmarking your 30 sites.

### Maricopa County–specific layer
Arizona child care licensing is **state-run, not county-delegated**, so there is no separate county child care license. But Maricopa adds:

- **Zoning / Conditional Use Permit** — required, varies by zone. Administered by **Maricopa County Planning & Development** in unincorporated areas, but by **the individual city** (Phoenix, Mesa, Chandler, Gilbert, Glendale, Scottsdale, Tempe, Peoria, Surprise, Goodyear) for incorporated sites. For a 30-site Phoenix-metro portfolio this means **you are managing ~8–12 different municipal zoning regimes**, not one.
- **Fire inspection** — required before opening; **annual re-inspection tied to license renewal**; local fire authority (county or city).
- **Business license** — county treasurer or city; **$2–$50 annually** by classification.
- **Environmental health permit** — required where food is prepared/served on site, under the **Maricopa County Environmental Health Code (MCEHC), revised September 10, 2025** ([MCEHC PDF](https://www.maricopa.gov/DocumentCenter/View/8075/P-14---Maricopa-County-Environmental-Health-Code-MCEHC-PDF)), with permit class and inspection frequency set by the county schedule **revised October 13, 2025** ([Permit Type, Class and Inspection Frequency](https://www.maricopa.gov/DocumentCenter/View/44074/Permit-Type-Class-and-Inspection-Frequency-PDF)). Maricopa publishes inspection results in a **public searchable database** ([Maricopa Environmental Services inspection portal](https://envapp.maricopa.gov/Inspection/FD-09474/INSP-059892-2026)) — a reputational exposure worth monitoring proactively across your kitchens.

## 2. SUBSIDY / CCDF — DES Child Care Assistance

**Lead agency:** Arizona Department of Economic Security (DES), Division of Child Care. **Director: Michael Wisehart** ([DES Director bio](https://des.az.gov/about-des/des-directors-bio)).

### The waitlist — the dominant fact in Arizona child care policy
DES **reinstated the Child Care Assistance waiting list on August 1, 2024** ([AZCIR, Aug 1, 2024](https://azcir.org/news/2024/08/01/az-reinstates-state-child-care-waitlist-for-families-seeking-aid/); [AZFamily, Aug 2, 2024](https://www.azfamily.com/2024/08/02/wait-list-is-back-thousands-families-applying-child-care-assistance/)). *(Note: the reinstatement was 2024, not 2025 — correcting the premise in the brief.)*

Growth trajectory:

| Date | Children on waitlist | Families |
|---|---|---|
| Aug 2024 (reinstatement) | ~2,000 | — |
| Feb 28, 2025 | 2,607 | 1,587 |
| June 5, 2026 | ~12,800 | ~7,600 |
| **June 26, 2026** | **13,244** | **7,916** |
| **July 2026** | **~13,500** | — |

Sources: [Children's Action Alliance](https://azchildren.org/news-and-events/well-thats-scary-child-care-crisis-in-arizona/); [AZPM, June 5, 2026](https://news.azpm.org/p/newsfeature/2026/6/5/230052-az-childcare-landscape-in-crisis-as-assistance-waitlist-approaches-12800-kids/); [DES "How to Apply" page, current](https://des.az.gov/services/child-and-family/child-care/how-apply-for-child-care-assistance); [Arizona Capitol Times, July 29, 2026](https://azcapitoltimes.com/news/2026/07/29/arizona-childcare-assistance-waitlist-increasing-fast/).

That is a **575% increase since reinstatement** per the Capitol Times. Advocates put the cost of clearing it at **$160 million**; the legislature has appropriated roughly **$90 million over two years**.

**Waitlist exemptions** (these families bypass the list entirely — they are your reliable subsidy channel): Jobs-program referrals; Cash Assistance recipients maintaining employment; Transitional Child Care (former Cash Assistance); **DCS or Tribal Child Welfare referrals** with documented case-plan need; siblings in households already receiving DCS-referred care; families with a DCS/tribal referral within the prior 30 days. ([DES CCA-1337A Waiting List Information](https://des.az.gov/sites/default/files/dl/CCA-1337A.pdf))

> **Operational implication:** DCS-referred placements are the only subsidy stream in Arizona currently immune to the waitlist. Sites with DCS relationships have a materially more stable subsidy census. This is worth a deliberate business-development push.

### Eligibility
- **Entry threshold: 165% of the Federal Poverty Level.** ([DES](https://des.az.gov/services/child-and-family/child-care/how-apply-for-child-care-assistance); [AZPM](https://news.azpm.org/p/newsfeature/2026/6/5/230052-az-childcare-landscape-in-crisis-as-assistance-waitlist-approaches-12800-kids/))
- Children **birth through age 12**, Arizona residents, parent in an eligible activity (employment, education/training, or homelessness/DV-shelter circumstances).
- Applications processed in up to 30 days; apply via the A-to-Z portal.
- 165% FPL is well below the federal ceiling of 85% State Median Income — Arizona is leaving significant federally-permitted eligibility on the table, which is why only **~16% of eligible Arizona children** actually receive a subsidy (18,000 children served). ([First Five Years Fund, Arizona, updated July 29, 2026](https://www.ffyf.org/states/arizona/))

### Reimbursement rates — CURRENT, effective November 1, 2025
([DES CCA-1227A, Maximum Reimbursement Rates](https://des.az.gov/sites/default/files/dl/CCA-1227A.pdf))

| Age group | Base daily rate | Quality rate (3–5 star, +40%) |
|---|---|---|
| Infant (under 1) | **$64.15** | **$89.81** |
| Toddler (1–2) | **$48.70** | **$68.18** |
| Preschool (3–5) | **$42.00** | **$58.80** |
| School-age, summer | **$35.00** | **$49.00** |
| School-age, school year | **$25.00** | **$35.00** |

**Methodology — and its central weakness:** rates are set at the **75th percentile of the 2022 Market Rate Survey for infants under 1**, and only the **50th percentile of the 2022 MRS for ages 1–12**. Two problems for providers: (a) the 50th-percentile benchmark for everything above infancy means the subsidy is calibrated to the *median* of a depressed market, and (b) **DES published a 2024 Market Rate Survey** ([2024 AZ Child Care Market Rate Survey](https://des.az.gov/sites/default/files/media/2024-AZ-Child-Care-Market-Rate-Survey.pdf)) but the rates in effect are still keyed to **2022** data — a roughly **3–4 year lag** against a period of significant wage and cost inflation.

**Quality differential:** a **40% rate increase** for providers rated **3, 4, or 5 stars** in Quality First, or meeting Head Start standards. This is the single largest revenue lever available to you in the subsidy channel — on infants it is worth **$25.66/day** per subsidized child. Hobbs's FY2027 executive budget explicitly funds "**$44.8M GF ... to maintain a 40% high quality enhancement**" ([FY2027 Executive Budget release, Jan 16, 2026](https://azgovernor.gov/office-arizona-governor/news/2026/01/governor-katie-hobbs-releases-fy2027-executive-budget-proposal)) — confirming the differential is a discretionary, appropriation-dependent policy, **not** a permanent entitlement. It is exposed in any downturn budget.

### Enrollment vs. attendance payment — Arizona pays on ATTENDANCE
Arizona pays the daily rate "**when attending for 15 minutes or more**" ([CCA-1227A](https://des.az.gov/sites/default/files/dl/CCA-1227A.pdf)). That is an **attendance-based** model, not the enrollment-based model the 2024 federal rule pushed. Arizona is **not** among the 25 states + D.C. that adopted enrollment-based payment. Revenue risk from absenteeism sits with you.

### Family copay and the 7% cap — MATERIALLY CHANGED IN 2026
The March 2024 CCDF Final Rule required states to cap family copays at **7% of income**, pay providers based on **enrollment**, pay **prospectively**, and use **grants/contracts** for some direct services.

**All four mandates were rescinded** by the federal **"Restoring Flexibility in the Child Care and Development Fund" final rule, published May 12, 2026, effective July 13, 2026.** ([Federal Register, 2026-09382](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/12/2026-09382/restoring-flexibility-in-the-child-care-and-development-fund-ccdf); [NAEYC analysis](https://www.naeyc.org/our-work/public-policy-advocacy/2026-ccdf-final-rule))

Under the 2026 rule, states may now: set copays without the 7% ceiling (subject only to the statutory "not a barrier" standard and sliding-scale requirement); pay on an **85% attendance threshold** or up to 5 absences/month or an alternative with justification; and pay **prospectively OR within 21 days of invoice**. No new compliance deadlines were imposed; HHS will address transitional waivers by future guidance.

**Practical Arizona reading:** the federal floor under Arizona's subsidy design just dropped. Arizona's attendance-based payment and its copay structure now face **less federal pressure to improve** — and, in a budget crunch, **more room to deteriorate**. Copay amounts are set in the DES **Gross Monthly Income Eligibility Chart and Fee Schedule, CCA-0229A-FY26, effective August 1, 2025** ([DES digital library entry](https://des.az.gov/digital-library/child-care-assistance-gross-monthly-income-eligibility-chart-and-fee-schedule-fy)) — the PDF itself is robots-blocked to automated retrieval; **pull it manually**. National benchmark: Arizona's average CCDBG copay is reported at **$35/month** ([FFYF](https://www.ffyf.org/states/arizona/)).

### CCDF State Plan
Arizona's operative plan is the **CCDF State Plan FFY 2025–2027** ([DES plan PDF](https://des.az.gov/sites/default/files/media/AZ-CCDF-State-Plan-FFY-2025-2027.pdf)), with **Amendment 1 approved March 26, 2025**, covering eligibility/enrollment barrier procedures, payment rate effective dates, and a transfer of fraud-investigation/recovery administration — all effective **August 1, 2024** ([Amendment 1](https://des.az.gov/sites/default/files/media/AZ-CCDF-State-Plan-FFY-2025-2027-Amendment-1.pdf)). **UNCONFIRMED:** whether DES has filed or plans an amendment responding to the July 13, 2026 federal rule. This is a high-value item to monitor.

## 3. FIRST THINGS FIRST — the structurally decaying asset

**What it is:** Created by **Proposition 203 (2006)**, funded by a dedicated tobacco tax, governed by the **Arizona Early Childhood Development and Health Board**, serving children birth–5. ([JLBC Prop 203 summary](https://www.azjlbc.gov/ballot/06prop203summ.pdf); [FTF governance](https://www.firstthingsfirst.org/governance/board/)) **CEO: Melinda Gulick** (joined ~2022). ([KJZZ, April 20, 2026](https://www.kjzz.org/the-show/2026-04-20/voter-approved-tobacco-tax-doesnt-apply-to-vapes-its-hurting-arizonas-early-childhood-agency))

**The revenue collapse — CONFIRMED and severe:**

| Metric | Figure |
|---|---|
| Year-one revenue (FY2008) | **~$180 million** |
| Most recent fiscal year | **~$89 million** |
| Decline over 20 years | **~50%** |
| Decline in last 3 years alone | **$32 million** |
| FTF share of state tobacco tax revenue | **~40%** |
| ASU estimate of revenue foregone by not taxing vapes | **$1.6 billion** |

([KJZZ, April 20, 2026](https://www.kjzz.org/the-show/2026-04-20/voter-approved-tobacco-tax-doesnt-apply-to-vapes-its-hurting-arizonas-early-childhood-agency); [12News I-Team investigation](https://www.12news.com/article/news/investigations/i-team/education-impact/arizona-shift-cigarettes-to-vaping-costing-early-childhood-programs-millions/75-8ab38276-b76a-4bd9-88cd-094663fd8406))

**Cause:** Prop 203 taxed cigarettes, cavendish, chewing tobacco and cigars. Vaping entered the market in **2007 — one year after the vote** — and is not covered. Every smoker who converts to vaping is a direct revenue loss to FTF.

**Legislative attempts to fix it:** FTF has sought a statutory fix for **two consecutive sessions without bipartisan support** ([KJZZ](https://www.kjzz.org/the-show/2026-04-20/voter-approved-tobacco-tax-doesnt-apply-to-vapes-its-hurting-arizonas-early-childhood-agency)). The vehicle in 2025 was **HB 2778**, a luxury tax on nicotine and vapor products directing **60% to First Things First / 40% to the General Fund** — it **did not receive a hearing in its chamber of origin** ([Children's Action Alliance legislation tracker](https://azchildren.org/news-and-events/category/legislation/)). Separately, the Senate advanced vape/e-cigarette **regulation** (not taxation) in May 2026 ([Arizona Capitol Times, May 28, 2026](https://azcapitoltimes.com/news/2026/05/28/senate-advances-new-regulations-for-vapes-and-e-cigarettes/)).

**Attempts to redirect FTF funds:** Because Prop 203 was voter-enacted, FTF's revenue is protected by the **Voter Protection Act** (Ariz. Const. art. IV, pt. 1, §1(6)(C)) — the legislature cannot sweep or repurpose it without a three-fourths supermajority *and* a furtherance-of-purpose finding, or a return to the ballot. Historically this has made FTF the target of ballot-referral attempts rather than statutory sweeps. **UNCONFIRMED:** no 2025 or 2026 referral to redirect FTF funds surfaced in my research. Treat FTF's *revenue base* as eroding but its *legal protection* as intact.

**FY2026/FY2027 FTF budget figures:** **UNCONFIRMED.** The FTF funding page returned 404s and board-approved regional funding plans were not retrievable within budget. The ~$89M most-recent-fiscal-year figure from KJZZ is the best available anchor. **Recommend pulling the FTF 2025 Annual Report** (referenced on [firstthingsfirst.org](https://www.firstthingsfirst.org/)) and the board's FY2027 funding plan directly.

**Bright spot — new federal money:** Arizona was one of **23 states** awarded a **Preschool Development Grant Birth Through Five Systems Building Grant of $8.4 million**, administered through the Arizona Department of Education. About **$500,000** will let roughly **60 additional providers** join Quality First as **Rating Only** participants. **Beginning July 2027, Rating Only participants at 3+ stars become eligible for Quality First Scholarships** previously reserved for Full Support participants. Targeted at high-poverty ZIP codes and providers serving infants, toddlers, and children with special needs. ([FTF, Feb 26, 2026](https://www.firstthingsfirst.org/2026/02/newly-awarded-grant-opens-more-access-to-quality-first/))

## 4. STATE PRE-K AND THE ESA PROGRAM

### State-funded pre-K
Arizona has **no universal or broadly-funded state pre-K program**. Public preschool is delivered through a patchwork: federal **Head Start / Early Head Start** (16,500 children — **9% of EHS-eligible, 26% of HS-eligible**), district-run preschool, ECE special education, and **First Things First Quality First Scholarships**. ([FFYF Arizona](https://www.ffyf.org/states/arizona/)) Arizona is thus **near the bottom nationally in state pre-K investment** — which is precisely why the private market, including yours, carries so much of the 3-5 load, and why FTF scholarships matter disproportionately.

Discrete FY2026/FY2027 appropriations rather than a program: **$3M out-of-school-time grant program** (ages 5–12, 30% rural set-aside), **$1.5M rural child development center construction in Taylor, Navajo County**, **$3.8M school meals**. ([CAA](https://azchildren.org/news-and-events/children-and-family-victories-this-legislative-session-and-in-the-arizona-budget/); [RAA](https://www.ruralazaction.org/post/2026-arizona-legislative-session-recap))

### ESA (Empowerment Scholarship Accounts) — the answer matters and it is restrictive
Arizona runs the nation's largest **universal** school-choice voucher program. **For preschool, the door is mostly closed.**

**Per the Arizona Department of Education ([ESA Eligibility Requirements](https://www.azed.gov/esa/eligibility-requirements); [ESA Parent Handbook SY2025-26](https://www.azed.gov/sites/default/files/2025/06/ESA%202025-2026%20Handbook.pdf)):**

- The general rule: a student must be **at least five years of age as of January 1 of the contract year** to be eligible as an entering kindergartener. **Typically-developing preschoolers ages 3–4 CANNOT participate.**
- **The one exception — preschoolers with disabilities.** A child **age 3 or older** (including children "within ninety days of their third birthday") who is **not yet kindergarten age** qualifies with a **current MET/Evaluation Report, IEP, or 504 Plan issued by an Arizona public school**. This category is **exempt from the 45-day prior public-school enrollment requirement** that gates other pathways.

**What this means for a for-profit operator:**

1. **ESA is not a general revenue channel for your 3s and 4s.** Do not model universal ESA uptake into preschool tuition.
2. **ESA IS a viable channel for a defined niche:** preschool-age children with a public-school-issued MET/IEP/504. To capture it you need (a) awareness among families that the disability pathway exists, (b) referral relationships with district ECE evaluation teams who issue the MET, and (c) the operational ability to accept ESA payment (ClassWallet vendor enrollment, ADE reporting).
3. **The 5-by-January-1 kindergarten rule creates a real ESA-funded pre-K/TK opportunity** for older 4s and young 5s who are ESA-eligible as entering kindergarteners. If any of your Phoenix-metro sites operate a kindergarten or transitional-K program, **that program can be ESA-funded** where the typical preschool cannot. This is the most under-exploited ESA angle for your business model. **Verify current award amounts and vendor requirements with ADE.**
4. **Award amounts: UNCONFIRMED** — the ADE eligibility page does not publish them. Base awards have historically tracked ~90% of state per-pupil charter funding with substantial disability-category uplifts; **confirm current figures directly.**

## 5. QUALITY RATING SYSTEM — Quality First

**Operator:** First Things First. **Scale: ~1,100–1,300 participating programs**; **~87% of enrolled providers meet or exceed quality standards** (3+ stars). ([Quality First about page](https://www.qualityfirstaz.com/about/); [FTF, Feb 2026](https://www.firstthingsfirst.org/2026/02/newly-awarded-grant-opens-more-access-to-quality-first/))

**Structure — two participation tracks:**
- **Full Support:** assessment, rating, coordination **plus** coaching, child care health consultation, and **funding**.
- **Rating Only:** assessment, rating, coordination; program remains on a **waitlist for full support** services.

**Rating scale:** **1–5 stars**; **3 stars and above = "meets or exceeds quality standards."** Participation in Quality First is **free** to the provider. ([Quality First about](https://www.qualityfirstaz.com/about/))

**Enrollment process:** apply → coordinator contacts within 15 days of selection → assessor observes and issues star rating → coach and program build a customized improvement plan.

**Financial incentives tied to rating** ([Quality First financial incentives](https://www.qualityfirstaz.com/participant-benefits/funding/)):
- Materials, equipment, and **facility improvements** — books, toys, furniture, outdoor equipment, and capital-ish items including **shelving, fencing, painting, carpet**.
- **Professional development** funds — conferences, online training, professional memberships.
- **Staff recognition/retention** funds.
- **Quality First Scholarships** — direct tuition subsidy for low-income families with children birth–5; reported through azftfscholarships.org.
- *Incentives "vary based on your star rating."*

**The money tie that matters most:** the **DES 40% subsidy rate enhancement is conditioned on a 3-, 4-, or 5-star Quality First rating** (or Head Start standards) ([CCA-1227A](https://des.az.gov/sites/default/files/dl/CCA-1227A.pdf)). Quality First is therefore not a soft branding exercise in Arizona — **it is the gate on your highest-value subsidy revenue.** Any Sunrise site at 1–2 stars, or not participating at all, is leaving ~40% of subsidy revenue on the table at that site.

**Watch item:** effective **July 2027**, Rating Only participants at 3+ stars become **scholarship-eligible** — expanding the value of the low-burden track. ([FTF](https://www.firstthingsfirst.org/2026/02/newly-awarded-grant-opens-more-access-to-quality-first/))

## 9. BUSINESS-CLIMATE ISSUES FOR A FOR-PROFIT OPERATOR

### Workforce and wages — the primary margin pressure
**Arizona's minimum wage is constitutionally indexed to CPI** under **Proposition 206 (2016), the Fair Wages and Healthy Families Act**. The Industrial Commission of Arizona resets it each **January 1** based on CPI. ([ICA minimum wage page](https://www.azica.gov/labor-minimum-wage-main-page); [JLBC Prop 206 fiscal analysis](https://www.azjlbc.gov/ballot/16novprop206fn.pdf))

| Year | Standard | Tipped |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | $14.35 | — |
| 2025 | $14.70 | — |
| **2026 (eff. Jan 1, 2026)** | **$15.15** | **$12.15** |
| 2027 | **UNCONFIRMED** — announced ~Sept/Oct 2026 | — |

([Inflection HR](https://www.inflectionhr.com/blog/arizona-minimum-wage-everything-you-need-to-know); corroborated by [Hirex](https://gethirex.com/minimum-wage/arizona), [KGUN9](https://www.kgun9.com/news/community-inspired-journalism/midtown-news/new-year-new-laws-az-minimum-wage-increases-in-2026); note [one source](https://remotelaws.com/minimum-wage/arizona/) still lists $14.70 for 2026 — **treat $15.15 as correct but verify against the official [ICA 2026 minimum wage poster](https://www.azica.gov/labor-minimum-wage-main-page)**.)

**Three compounding pressures:**
1. **Automatic annual increases with no legislative off-ramp.** Prop 206 is voter-protected; the legislature cannot pause it. Build 3–4% annual wage escalation into every Arizona pro forma indefinitely.
2. **Prop 206 also mandates earned paid sick time** — a separate and often-underbudgeted cost for a high-headcount, high-turnover workforce.
3. **The wage floor rises faster than the subsidy rate.** Reimbursement rates are pegged to a **2022** market rate survey while wages have risen from $13.85 (2022) to $15.15 (2026) — roughly **9.4%** — with **no corresponding subsidy rate rebase**. This is the structural margin squeeze in Arizona's subsidy channel, and it is the single strongest, most defensible advocacy argument you have.

**Local wage ordinances:** **Flagstaff and Tucson** maintain separate, higher local minimum wages. **Neither is in Maricopa County** — no Phoenix-metro city currently imposes a separate wage floor, so your ~30 sites are on the state rate. ([Inflection HR](https://www.inflectionhr.com/blog/arizona-minimum-wage-everything-you-need-to-know))

**Workforce supports:** Arizona launched a **Bright Futures AZ Scholarship with $1M for early childhood educators' own child care needs** (May 2025) ([Hoodline](https://hoodline.com/2025/05/arizona-launches-bright-futures-az-scholarship-with-1m-for-early-childhood-educators-child-care-needs/)) — a retention tool your HR team should be routing staff toward. Quality First also funds professional development and staff recognition for participating programs.

### Tax treatment
- **No state child care tax credit or employer child care incentive** identified as enacted (**UNCONFIRMED but likely accurate**).
- **Federal §45F employer-provided child care credit was expanded effective tax year 2026** ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/employer-provided-child-care-credit-tax-year-2026-and-later)). **This is a demand-side opportunity, not a cost:** Phoenix-metro employers now have a materially better federal credit for subsidizing employee child care. A B2B employer-partnership channel — direct contracts or tuition-benefit arrangements with large Maricopa employers — is the most attractive growth vector in this landscape, precisely because it is **insulated from the DES waitlist**. Third-party intermediaries are already active in Arizona on this model ([TriShare](https://trisharechildcare.com/states/arizona)).

Fire inspection is required pre-opening and **annually as a license-renewal condition**.
