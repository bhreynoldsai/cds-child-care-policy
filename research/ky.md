# KENTUCKY — Child Care Policy, Regulation & Funding Profile
**As of August 3, 2026** | Prepared for a multi-state for-profit operator

**Headline:** Kentucky just completed the most consequential child care legislative cycle in its history. **HB 6 (2026), Acts Ch. 146** — a Chamber-brokered, 27-sponsor omnibus — became law **without the Governor's signature on 4/14/26**, codifying the child care worker subsidy, privatizing ECCAP administration, authorizing microcenters, and freezing agency discretion over All STARS. Simultaneously, the **FY2027–28 budget (HB 500, Acts Ch. 168)** held child care flat in an austerity biennium. The regulatory posture is loosening; the money is not growing.

---

## 1. LICENSING & REGULATION

**Agency:** Cabinet for Health and Family Services (CHFS) → Dept. for Community Based Services (DCBS) → **Division of Child Care (DCC)** ([chfs.ky.gov/agencies/dcbs/dcc](https://www.chfs.ky.gov/agencies/dcbs/dcc/Pages/default.aspx)). Note a separate **Division of Regulated Child Care** sits in the Office of Inspector General ([link](https://www.chfs.ky.gov/agencies/os/oig/drcc/Pages/default.aspx)) — a two-agency split worth mapping for compliance escalation.

**Core regulations — both amended effective 2/13/2025** (the most recent substantive licensing revision cycle):

| Reg | Subject | Last amended |
|---|---|---|
| [922 KAR 2:090](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/kar/titles/922/002/090/) | Child-care center licensure | **2/13/2025** (prior: 6/24/2021, 7/29/2020, 7/18/2018) |
| [922 KAR 2:120](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/kar/titles/922/002/120/) | Health & safety standards; ratios | **2/13/2025** (51 Ky.R. 158, 1140; filed w/ LRC 11/13/2024) |
| [922 KAR 2:160](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/kar/titles/922/002/160/) | Child Care Assistance Program | **11/18/2024** (51 Ky.R. 420) |
| [922 KAR 2:270](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/kentucky/922-KAR-2-270) | Kentucky All STARS | 7/18/2018 |
| [922 KAR 2:280](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/kar/titles/922/002/280/) | Background checks | 3/11/2024 (technical) |

### Ratios and group size (922 KAR 2:120 §2(2))

| Age | Ratio | Max group size* |
|---|---|---|
| Infant (0–12 mo) | 1:5 | 10 |
| Toddler 12–24 mo | 1:6 | 12 |
| Toddler 24–36 mo | 1:10 | 20 |
| Preschool 3–4 yr | 1:12 | 24 |
| Preschool 4–5 yr | 1:14 | 28 |
| School-age 5–7 yr | 1:15 | 30 |
| School-age 7+ (before/after school) | 1:25 | 30 |
| School-age 7+ (full day) | 1:20 | 30 |

\*Max group size **applies only to Type I centers.** Key operational rules: **the age of the youngest child in the group sets the ratio and group size**; staff members' own children count in ratio; centers must maintain a **written procedure to preserve ratios during transitions and breaks** — a common citation vector.

**Commercial read:** Kentucky's infant ratio (1:5) is mid-pack and *less* restrictive than neighbors like Illinois (1:4); the 1:14 four-year-old ratio and 1:20/1:25 school-age ratios are relatively generous and materially favorable to unit economics.

### Facility types (922 KAR 2:090)
- **Type I** — 4+ children in a **nonresidential** setting (the multi-site operator's category).
- **Type II** — 7–12 children in the **licensee's primary residence**.
- **Child-care microcenter** — NEW category created by HB 6 (see §7).

### Staff qualifications & training (922 KAR 2:090)
- **Type I director:** one of eight credential pathways, from a master's in child development down to **three years of direct experience** with children in approved settings. The experience pathway is a meaningful hiring flexibility.
- **Staff with supervisory authority:** 6 hrs orientation within 3 months (federal health/safety content); **9 hrs early care education training in year one** (incl. 1.5 hrs pediatric abusive head trauma); **15 annual hours thereafter**, with the 1.5-hr head-trauma module every 5 years.
- At least one person on duty must hold current CPR and first aid.
- **NEW (HB 6):** **3 hrs/yr special-needs training for center staff** (2 hrs for family child-care), **effective 10/1/2026** — budget for this in FY27 training plans.

### Background checks (922 KAR 2:280)
Kentucky National Background Check Program. Covers owners, operators, directors, employees, and anyone with supervisory control or **unsupervised contact**; adult household members in family homes and small centers.
- **Components:** child abuse/neglect central registry; National Sex Offender Public Website; state sex offender registry; **fingerprint-based state police + FBI checks with rap-back monitoring**; and, if the applicant lived elsewhere in the prior 5 years, **out-of-state criminal, sex offender registry, and child abuse registry checks** (a real hiring-velocity drag for border markets — Cincinnati/Louisville/Tri-State).
- **Timing:** complete **before** becoming a staff member; **recheck every 5 years**; new checks after **180+ days** separation. Cabinet issues eligibility notice **within 45 days**.
- **Disqualifiers:** drug felonies within 5 yrs of discharge; sex/violent crimes per KRS 17.165; child abuse/neglect/exploitation; fraud/embezzlement/theft/forgery (administrative roles); pending charges or open warrants.
- **Rehabilitative review** available 10+ years post-discharge for non-violent felonies (child-related offenses excepted).

### Enforcement — direction of travel is toward provider protection
**HB 6 imported the substance of SB 160 (Sen. Danny Carroll) as a Senate floor amendment.** SB 160 itself died in House Appropriations & Revenue ([record](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/record/26rs/sb160.html)) but its content rode HB 6. It amends **KRS 199.896** to:
- Set explicit factors governing when the Cabinet may issue a correction plan vs. a **directed** plan vs. suspension/revocation;
- **Bar a single violation of a directed plan from triggering suspension** unless independently justified;
- Give centers **5 business days** to produce documentation on Cabinet request;
- Require **weekly support contacts** for preliminary-licensed centers;
- Apply **retroactively to pending enforcement actions**.

HB 6 separately establishes **informal dispute resolution for licensure deficiencies**. Also: centers with a prior denial/revocation face **semi-annual inspections during a 2-year probationary period** (922 KAR 2:090).

> **Action item:** If any Kentucky site has a pending or recent enforcement action, the retroactivity clause in the KRS 199.896 amendments is directly usable. Have counsel review open matters against the new standard.

## 2. SUBSIDY / CCDF — Child Care Assistance Program (CCAP)

**Lead agency:** CHFS/DCBS/Division of Child Care. Governing reg: [922 KAR 2:160](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/kentucky/922-KAR-2-160). CCDF plan: [FFY 2025–2027 Approved State Plan](https://www.chfs.ky.gov/agencies/dcbs/dcc/Documents/stateplan20252027.pdf).

### Eligibility — **85% SMI, verified current**
Kentucky sits at the **federal maximum**. 922 KAR 2:160 §8(1): a family qualifies when *"the family's income is less than or equal to eighty-five (85) percent of the SMI as prepared by the U.S. Census Bureau."*

**History:** originally 160% FPL → **200% FPL (Jan 2022)** → **85% SMI (July 2022)**, funded initially with ARPA and sustained since ([ECE State Policy](https://www.ecestatepolicy.org/solution-in-action/kentucky-child-care-assistance-program)). The expansion added ~2,999 families / 4,425 children initially, ~7,800 children from 5,300 families cumulatively.

**Current thresholds (effective 10/1/2025)** per [CHFS CCAP fact sheet](https://www.chfs.ky.gov/agencies/dcbs/dcc/Documents/dcc11319.pdf) — monthly gross:
- Family of 2: **$4,862** | Family of 3: **$6,005** | Family of 4: **$7,149** | +$215 per member above 8.

### Transitional / phase-out
§9(6)(a): a child **continues to receive assistance for 6 months after becoming income-ineligible** — but **providers receive only 50% of the standard rate** during that window. Model this: it is a margin cliff on a defined subset of your subsidy census, not a full loss.

### Payment based on **enrollment**, not attendance
§17(1)(a): *"Payment under the CCAP shall be made to a registered, certified, or licensed provider based on the enrollment of the child, per the care schedule on the DCC-94."* Kentucky adopted this in the ARPA era and has retained it — **materially favorable and ahead of the 2024 federal rule.** Estimated annual cost of this policy: **~$20M** ([KCEP](https://kypolicy.org/child-care-funding-2024-2026-budget/)).

### Copays and the federal 7% cap
§11(3)(a): a **daily** copay chart by gross monthly income and household size, from **$0** (income $0–$1,599.99) to a **$25 maximum** (over $8,199.99/mo). *"The maximum copayment for an eligible family with more than five (5) members shall be twenty-five (25) dollars."* **Copays are set at initial application and fixed for the 12-month eligibility period.** Copays are waived for very low income and for child-welfare-involved families. Enrollment fees are capped at **$100/family/year, once per 12 months**, for KTAP or child protection cases ([CHFS](https://www.chfs.ky.gov/agencies/dcbs/dcc/Pages/ccap.aspx)).

> **7% cap — UNCONFIRMED.** Kentucky's flat-dollar daily copay structure is *directionally* compliant (a $25/day max on an $8,200/mo household is well under 7%), but I could not retrieve an explicit CHFS or ACF statement certifying compliance with the 2024 CCDF final rule's 7%-of-income cap, nor a documented statewide 7% affordability test. **Verify directly with DCC.**

### Rates and methodology
- Rates are published in the **DCC-300, Kentucky Child Care Maximum Payment Rate Chart**, incorporated by reference at §10(1). Rates vary by **provider type** (licensed Type I/II, certified, registered relative), **child age band** (infant/toddler, preschool, school-age), and **care duration** (full day vs. part day).
- **Methodology:** rates were set at the **80th percentile of the state Market Rate Survey**, increased July 2021 ([ECE State Policy](https://www.ecestatepolicy.org/solution-in-action/kentucky-child-care-assistance-program)). Maintaining the 80th percentile costs **~$40M/yr** ([KCEP](https://kypolicy.org/child-care-funding-2024-2026-budget/)).
- **MAJOR FORWARD CHANGE — HB 6:** the Cabinet **must request ACF approval to use a cost-estimation model** (rather than market rate survey) to set payment rates for the **FY2028–2030 CCDF plan**. The model must incorporate *"provider type; hours and seasons of operation; child age range served; geography; level of quality."* This is the single most important medium-term rate-setting development in Kentucky. ([HB 6 text](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/recorddocuments/bill/26RS/hb6/bill.pdf))

> **UNCONFIRMED: current DCC-300 dollar rates.** I could not retrieve the live rate chart at any tested CHFS URL (multiple 404s). **Obtain the current DCC-300 directly from DCC** — this is the highest-value single missing data point for revenue modeling.

### The fiscal cliff context
ARPA delivered **~$330M/yr** to Kentucky child care through Sept. 2024: ~38% to CCDBG (~$130M/yr in state replacement need) and ~62% to stabilization grants (~$200M/yr). The $130M CCDBG replacement decomposes as: **$40M** rate maintenance at 80th percentile + **$40M** the 85% SMI expansion + **$20M** enrollment-based payment + **$30M** child care worker eligibility ([KCEP](https://kypolicy.org/child-care-funding-2024-2026-budget/)). State allocation for the 2024–2026 biennium was **$112.45M** ([ECE State Policy](https://www.ecestatepolicy.org/solution-in-action/kentucky-child-care-assistance-program)).

**Caseload:** ~42,000 children enrolled statewide; 35,717 children from 20,865 families as of Sept. 2023.

> **UNCONFIRMED: waitlist.** No CCAP waiting list was documented in the CCDF state plan excerpt or CHFS materials retrieved. Kentucky has not historically operated a CCAP waitlist. **Verify with DCC.**

## 3. ECCAP — Employee Child Care Assistance Partnership ⭐

**The distinctive Kentucky program, and the one with the clearest commercial play for you.** Created by **HB 499 (2022)**, codified at **[KRS 199.885](https://law.justia.com/codes/kentucky/chapter-199/section-199-885/)**, effective 4/8/2022. Program page: [CHFS ECCAP](https://www.chfs.ky.gov/agencies/dcbs/dcc/Pages/assistancepartnershipprogram.aspx).

### Structure — a three-way split
Cost is shared by **employee / employer / state**. CHFS's own example: employer contributes **$400/mo** toward a **$1,200/mo** child care cost; the state **matches the employer's $400**; the employee pays the remaining $400 — i.e., **the employer's dollar buys two dollars of tuition relief.**

### Match scale (KRS 199.885, as amended by HB 6)
| Employee household income | State match on employer contribution |
|---|---|
| ≤100% state median household income | up to **100%** |
| 100%–180% SMI | **–10% per each 20%** income increment above 100% |
| >180% SMI | **50%** (floor) |

The match never falls below 50%.

### Critical eligibility structure — ECCAP is a *middle-income* program
Per the [ECCAP employee packet](https://www.chfs.ky.gov/agencies/dcbs/dcc/Documents/eccapemployeepacket.pdf), household income must be **at or above 85% SMI** — **below that, the employee is referred to CCAP instead.** ECCAP and CCAP are mutually exclusive; an employee cannot receive both. 85% SMI monthly floors in the packet: family of 2 **$4,639**; of 3 **$5,731**; of 4 **$6,822**; of 5 **$7,914**; +$205/member above 8.

> Note the packet's 85% SMI figures ($6,822 for a family of 4) are **lower** than the CCAP fact sheet's 10/1/2025 figures ($7,149). The packet appears to be on an earlier SMI table. **Confirm which schedule ECCAP is currently applying.**

### Provider requirement — **All STARS participation is mandatory**
The child care provider must be **licensed/certified, operating in Kentucky, and participating in Kentucky All STARS.** Non-rated providers disqualify the employer from the match ([45F.org KY](https://45f.org/states/kentucky)). Your Kentucky centers must be All STARS-enrolled to be an eligible destination for ECCAP dollars. Providers cannot be a parent/stepparent/guardian/household member; private school tuition is excluded.

### Mechanics and deadlines
Three-part application via the **KICCS portal**: employer completes Section I → employee Section II → provider Section III. Paper alternative: **form DCC-600** (English/Spanish) to DCC.
- **New employer application deadline: May 17 annually**
- **Existing employer renewal deadline: April 2 annually**
- Fiscal year **July 1 – June 30**; state eligibility decision within **30 days**; employee has **10 business days** to return contracts; **30 days** to request an administrative hearing after adverse action.
- Multiple children: **payments apply to the youngest child first**, remainder to next oldest.

### Funding and set-asides
- **$2 million in matching funds appropriated for FY2026** ([45F.org](https://45f.org/states/kentucky); [KY Chamber](https://www.kychamber.com/childcare)). This is a **small pot** — first-come, first-served by contract processing order.
- **25% of the fund is reserved for small business employers** (KRS 199.885) — reduces the pool available to a large operator's corporate partners.
- **Administrative cost carve-out rises to 10%** beginning FY2026-27 (was 5% in FY22-23, 3% thereafter) — HB 6 raised this to fund the third-party administrator.
- Fund is **revolving; unspent balances carry forward** and do not lapse.
- **The state match is NOT taxable compensation** to the employee — KRS 199.885 expressly provides it *"shall not be considered compensation for an employee's service."* Strong selling point.

### HB 6 reforms — effective now
**By December 1, 2026, the Cabinet must contract with a private third-party administrator** to handle applications, eligibility verification, fund distribution, and reporting. The stated intent is to make ECCAP "easier for Kentucky employers and providers to utilize" — current uptake has been constrained by KICCS friction.

### How a multi-site operator uses ECCAP — three distinct plays
1. **As an employer (workforce play).** Enroll your own Kentucky corporate entity as a participating employer and contribute toward *your own staff's* child care at your own centers. Because the state matches up to 100%, an employer contribution to a teacher's tuition is effectively doubled. **But note:** most center staff will fall *below* 85% SMI and route to CCAP instead (see §4) — so this play targets directors, regional managers, and higher-paid roles.
2. **As a provider/destination (revenue play).** Ensure every Kentucky site is All STARS-enrolled, then market ECCAP eligibility to large local employers as a reason to steer their workforce to your centers. This is a differentiated enrollment funnel that non-rated competitors cannot offer.
3. **Stack with federal 45F.** Employer contributions can claim the federal **Section 45F** credit (40% large / 50% small business on qualified expenditures) *while also* receiving the ECCAP state match — a genuine double benefit. **OBBBA (2026)** expanded qualified 45F expenses to include **intermediary service providers, benefits administration services, and technology platforms** connecting employees to licensed providers ([45F.org](https://45f.org/states/kentucky)). Kentucky offers **no separate state employer child care tax credit** — ECCAP is the state vehicle.

> **Advocacy note:** The **Kentucky Collaborative on Child Care** (Chamber-convened, 2024) has explicitly stated it "will be working to strengthen ECCAP by advocating for key changes to make it easier for Kentucky employers and providers to utilize" ([Bottom Line, 1/28/26](https://kychamberbottomline.com/2026/01/28/national-research-institute-highlights-kentuckys-innovative-approach-to-child-care-affordability/)). The **Buffett Early Childhood Institute** nationally spotlighted the model. **The third-party administrator procurement (due 12/1/26) is an open, near-term influence point — and the appropriation level is the binding constraint worth lobbying.**

## 4. CHILD CARE WORKFORCE — Categorical CCAP Eligibility ✅ **NOW PERMANENT**

**This is Kentucky's single strongest recruitment tool, and HB 6 just moved it from budget-contingent policy into statute.**

### Mechanics
Under **922 KAR 2:160 §4(4)**: *"To the extent funds are available, a household shall have all earned and unearned income excluded from the eligibility determination if an applicant or responsible adult meets the requirements of subsection (1) of this section and has verified employment in a regulated licensed child-care center or certified family child-care home."*

Operationally: an employee of a licensed center working **20+ hours/week** is **automatically eligible for CCAP for their own children regardless of household income** ([Zaentz Early Education Initiative](https://zaentznavigator.gse.harvard.edu/states/kentucky/)). It is an **income disregard**, not a separate program — the family still must meet non-income technical requirements, and standard copays apply based on countable (i.e., zeroed) income, meaning most qualify at the **$0 copay** tier.

### Statutory codification — HB 6 §9, new **KRS 199.889**
> *"To the extent funds are available and to the extent allowable under federal law, the cabinet shall exclude all earned and unearned income from the eligibility determination for the Child Care Assistance Program if an applicant: 1. Meets all nonincome-related requirements for program eligibility as determined by the cabinet; and 2. a. Has verified employment in a regulated licensed type I or licensed type II child-care program, **a child-care microcenter program**, or a certified family child-care home; or b. Is the owner or operator of a certified family child-care home or licensed type II child-care program."*

**Three things to note:**
1. **It is now permanent statute** — previously it was ARPA-funded from 2022 and extended with state general funds through FY24, requiring repeated budget renewal. Confirmed as a HB 6 objective: "child care worker assistance program codification" ([Bottom Line, 2/17/26](https://kychamberbottomline.com/2026/02/17/child-care-reform-legislation-filed-to-support-working-families-in-kentucky/)).
2. **Microcenter employees are now included** — extending the benefit to the new category.
3. **The "to the extent funds are available" qualifier survives.** Codification does not appropriate. This remains a funding-contingent entitlement, and the FY27-28 budget did not increase child care funding (see §7).

### Scale and cost
- **3,200+ child care workers and 5,600 of their children** benefited in the first year ([Zaentz](https://zaentznavigator.gse.harvard.edu/states/kentucky/); [ECE State Policy](https://www.ecestatepolicy.org/solution-in-action/kentucky-child-care-assistance-program)).
- **Annual cost: ~$30 million** ([KCEP](https://kypolicy.org/child-care-funding-2024-2026-budget/)).

> **Commercial read:** This is worth several thousand dollars per year in effective compensation to a Kentucky teacher with young children, at zero employer cost. It should be a headline item in every Kentucky job posting and offer letter. **Verify with DCC that the "funds available" trigger has not been throttled in FY27** given the flat budget.

## 5. STATE PRE-K — Kentucky Preschool Program (KPP)

**Regulation:** [704 KAR 3:410](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/kar/titles/704/003/410/). Administered by the **Kentucky Department of Education**, Office of Special Education and Early Learning.

### Eligibility
- **Age:** 4 years old by **October 1** of the school year, and a resident of the district.
- **Income:** children **approved for free lunch under federal free lunch criteria** (≈130% FPL) — a narrow threshold. Districts may determine eligibility up to 4 months before school starts; once approved, eligibility runs the full school year.
- **Disability:** all age-eligible 3- and 4-year-olds with disabilities, regardless of income. Programs must provide adaptations and consider **increased staff or reduced group size** for children with disabilities.
- **Space-available flexibility:** children who do not qualify for free lunch **may still enroll if space is available** — a paid-seat pathway.

### Funding and reach
- **~$170 million for the Preschool Program over the FY27-28 biennium** ([KY Senate GOP](https://kysenaterepublicans.com/press-releases/2026/4/6/general-assembly-finalizes-disciplined-balanced-two-year-state-budget)). Prior-year benchmark: **$112.6M** state pre-K spending (2024) ([Zaentz](https://zaentznavigator.gse.harvard.edu/states/kentucky/)).
- **Flat funding:** per Prichard's analysis of HB 500, "the budget does not increase funding for preschool and extended school services, which remain at their **2019 level through 2028**" ([Prichard](https://prichardcommittee.org/the-house-just-voted-on-a-budget-check-out-the-changes/)).
- **Access is weak and declining.** Kentucky serves **26% of 4-year-olds and 8% of 3-year-olds**; enrollment **fell by ~900 children** year-over-year. Kentucky meets **8 of 10 NIEER quality benchmarks** — strong quality, poor access — and **lags Georgia, West Virginia, Florida, and Alabama**, all of which use **mixed-delivery** models ([Prichard, 5/13/26](https://prichardcommittee.org/new-data-show-kentucky-falling-behind-on-preschool-access/)).

### ⭐ Private provider partnership pathway
**704 KAR 3:410 expressly authorizes districts to contract** with "another school district, another public agency, **private school or preschool program**." For a private program receiving state funds, the contract must document the program:
- Is **separately incorporated from any religious institution**;
- Has a **nonsectarian board of directors**;
- Maintains **separate finances and pays reasonable rent**;
- Offers a **curriculum that is not religious in nature**.

**This is a live and under-exploited channel.** As a secular for-profit operator you clear the sectarian tests trivially. Prichard is actively campaigning for coordinated mixed-delivery expansion, and Kentucky's poor access ranking versus mixed-delivery neighbors creates the policy rationale. **Pursue district-level KPP contracts, particularly in Louisville/Jefferson, Lexington/Fayette, and Northern Kentucky.**

**Quality note:** State-funded preschool programs must participate in **Kentucky All STARS per 704 KAR 3:015**. KDE revised the preschool quality system for 2024-25 using Preschool Development Grant funds, adopting the **Teaching Pyramid Observation Tool (TPOT)** plus a Basic Care Checklist, rolling out in three cohorts (2024-25, 2025-26, 2026-27). Key dates: **January 10** classroom selection, **April 30** TPOT observations complete ([KDE](https://www.education.ky.gov/specialed/earlylearning/Pages/pqa.aspx)).

## 6. QUALITY RATING — Kentucky All STARS

**Regulation:** [922 KAR 2:270](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/kentucky/922-KAR-2-270) (effective 7/18/2018). Provider page: [CHFS All STARS](https://www.chfs.ky.gov/agencies/dcbs/dcc/Pages/kyallstars.aspx).

### Structure — five levels, four domains
Points across: **Family & Community Engagement (10 max)**, **Classroom & Instructional Quality (20 max)**, **Staff Qualifications & Professional Development (10 max)**, **Administrative & Leadership Practices (10 max)** — 50 points total.

| Level | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 1 | Automatic for all licensed providers in good standing; preliminary-license holders **must** remain here |
| 2 | Developmental screening PD for 50% of staff; environmental self-assessment; administrator + 50% of teaching staff complete **10 hrs PD** |
| 3 | Level 2 + environmental observation (**any score**) + **21–30 points** |
| 4 | Level 2 + environmental observation **≥4.0** + **31–40 points** |
| 5 | Level 2 + environmental observation **≥5.0** + **41–50 points** — "highest quality" |

Levels 3+ require **nationally standardized Environment Rating Scale** observation.

### Mandatory participation
*"A provider receiving public funds shall participate in STARS upon preliminary licensure or certification."* All providers with regular or preliminary licenses are auto-enrolled. **Providers receiving no public funds may opt out** via form **DCC-433**. Since CCAP and ECCAP both constitute public funds, **a multi-site operator serving any subsidy child is mandatorily in All STARS** — and ECCAP separately requires it.

### Tie to subsidy rates — **monthly per-child add-ons for CCAP children**
| Provider type | Level 3–5 range, child under 3 | Level 3–5 range, age 3+ |
|---|---|---|
| **Type I center** | **$25–$45/mo** | **$23–$43/mo** |
| Type II / family home | $20–$40/mo | $18–$38/mo |

Plus **initial achievement awards of $250–$5,000** depending on provider type and level. Level 5 providers are eligible for **initial, annual, and subsidy enrollment monetary incentives "while funding is available."**

> **Economics:** For a Type I center, moving from Level 3 to Level 5 is worth ~**$20–22/month per subsidy child** — roughly **$240–264/child/year**. On a 40-subsidy-child site that is ~$10K/yr, before achievement awards. Meaningful but not transformative; the stronger argument for Level 5 is **ECCAP eligibility and KPP contracting credibility**.

### ⭐ Recent changes — HB 6 puts All STARS under legislative control
HB 6 creates a **"quality-based graduated early care and education rating system program"** and:
- Requires CHFS to submit a **comprehensive modernization plan by October 1, 2026** (stakeholder engagement strategy + alignment with legislative intent);
- Requires **final recommendations by December 1, 2027**;
- **Prohibits the Cabinet from making administrative changes to the rating system without express General Assembly authorization**, except as required for federal compliance.

**This is a significant governance shift** — it freezes DCC's unilateral authority over All STARS and routes redesign through the legislature. For a multi-state operator, it means: (a) the current 922 KAR 2:270 framework is **stable through at least late 2027**, and (b) **the modernization plan process in fall 2026 is an open comment/influence window.**

## 7. 2025–2026 LEGISLATION & BUDGET

### Divided government dynamics
Governor **Andy Beshear (D)** faces Republican **supermajorities** in both chambers. Kentucky requires only a **constitutional majority** to override a veto, so overrides are routine — the 2026 session produced overrides on both the budget and HJR 50. Kentucky's **10-day veto recess** structure lets the legislature pass bills, absorb vetoes, and override in the final two days.

### 2025 Session (odd-year, 30-day)
No major standalone child care legislation was enacted. The session's health/human services energy went to **HB 695** (Medicaid overhaul — vetoed 3/26/25, **overridden 3/27/25**, House 80-20 / Senate 29-7, Acts Ch. 110) ([record](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/record/25rs/hb695.html)). The child care work was instead happening outside the chamber, in the **Kentucky Collaborative on Child Care** (convened by the Kentucky Chamber in **June 2024**, 40+ stakeholders, 18 months) — which produced the 2026 package.

> **UNCONFIRMED:** A comprehensive list of 2025 child care bills that failed. The [KYA bill tracker](https://kyyouth.org/bill-tracker/) is the authoritative source but was inaccessible (robots.txt). Recommend pulling it directly.

## 8. 2027 SESSION OUTLOOK

### Calendar
The 2027 Regular Session is an **odd-year, 30-legislative-day session**. Per Ky. Const. §36, odd-year sessions convene the **first Tuesday after the first Monday in January** — **January 5, 2027** — and must adjourn **no later than March 30**. *(Derived from constitutional rule; LRC's 2027 session calendar page was not retrievable — **confirm exact dates with LRC.**)* Odd-year sessions require a **3/5 supermajority to raise revenue**, and no biennial budget is written — so 2027 is a **policy session, not a money session**.

## 10. BUSINESS-CLIMATE ISSUES FOR A FOR-PROFIT OPERATOR

### Workforce & wages — **Kentucky's strongest asset for you**
- **The categorical CCAP eligibility for child care employees (KRS 199.889) is the best-in-class recruitment tool in the region** and is now permanent statute. Free child care for staff with young children, at zero employer cost. Lead with it.
- **Kentucky has no state minimum wage above the federal $7.25** — wage floor pressure is market-driven, not statutory. *(General knowledge; verify current status.)*
- **Early Childhood Development Scholarships:** up to **$6,751/semester** for eligible workers pursuing credentials or degrees.
- **Three registered apprenticeship pathways** with progressive wage increases, mentor stipends, and milestone payments covered by the program ([Zaentz](https://zaentznavigator.gse.harvard.edu/states/kentucky/)).
- **Tobacco Settlement Fund:** ~$7.4M/yr supporting early childhood.
- **Director credential flexibility** — the 3-years-experience pathway for Type I directors is a real advantage over states requiring degrees.
- **Friction:** the 5-year-lookback out-of-state background check requirement slows hiring in Louisville, Northern Kentucky (Cincinnati metro), and the Tri-State — build lead time into staffing models.

### Tax treatment
- **No Kentucky employer child care tax credit exists.** ECCAP is the state's vehicle, structured as a **cash matching grant, not a credit** — which is better for cash flow (funds flow **directly to the provider**, not deferred to tax filing) ([45F.org](https://45f.org/states/kentucky)).
- **Federal §45F stacks with ECCAP** — 40% (large) / 50% (small business) credit on qualified expenditures *plus* the state match. **OBBBA (2026)** expanded qualified 45F expenses to include intermediary service providers, benefits administration, and technology platforms.
- **ECCAP state match is not taxable compensation** to the employee (KRS 199.885).
- **SB 191 / HB 6 kindergarten readiness pilot** offers **$2,000 per-child incentive payments *or refundable tax credits*** to providers achieving kindergarten readiness outcomes — a new, if small and pilot-scale, performance revenue stream beginning 8/1/2027.
- **Certified Child Care Community Designation Program** (2024 Ky. Acts ch. 88, expanded by HB 6) — funded through the **Cabinet for Economic Development**; a local/regional designation with employer engagement. Standardized application materials due 1/1/2028. Worth positioning in target counties.

> **UNCONFIRMED:** Kentucky sales/property tax treatment of child care facilities, and any local occupational license tax exposure (Kentucky's county/city **occupational license taxes on payroll** are a real and often-overlooked multi-jurisdiction compliance burden — Louisville Metro, Lexington-Fayette, and Northern Kentucky counties all levy them at differing rates). **Have tax counsel scope this by site.**

### Zoning & siting
> **UNCONFIRMED.** No state-level child care zoning preemption was identified in the research. Kentucky zoning is governed by **KRS Chapter 100** and is fundamentally **local**. Type I centers in nonresidential settings are generally conditional or permitted uses in commercial zones, but this varies by planning unit. **The HJR 50 Auditor's regulatory review (due 12/1/2027) explicitly covers "statutes, administrative regulations, and agency policies affecting the establishment and operation" of child care — siting and zoning barriers are squarely in scope. This is the venue to raise local zoning friction.**

### Liability & insurance
> **UNCONFIRMED.** No Kentucky-specific child care liability statute, damages cap, or insurance mandate was surfaced. Relevant adjacent items found: HB 6 requires **on-premises access for external diagnosticians and therapists** supporting IFSPs/IEPs (a new third-party-on-site exposure to address with your carrier), and **KRS 199.8951** permits centers to stock **epinephrine auto-injectors** with required parental notification and emergency action plans. Kentucky abolished its medical review panel regime and has generally *not* enacted broad tort reform. **Scope with your broker; not resolvable from public policy sources.**

### Compliance data transparency — a competitive factor
CHFS publishes **Child Care Inspection and Abuse/Injury Data** publicly ([DCC](https://www.chfs.ky.gov/agencies/dcbs/dcc/Pages/default.aspx)). Combined with HB 6's new **monthly supply reporting** (from 12/1/2026) and **real capacity tracking** (from 7/1/2027), Kentucky is moving toward a far more transparent market. Expect competitor capacity and citation histories to become readily comparable — and yours to be equally visible.
