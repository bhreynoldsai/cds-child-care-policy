# ARKANSAS — Child Care Policy, Regulation & Funding Landscape
**Government-Affairs Intelligence Profile | Current as of August 3, 2026**
*Prepared for a multi-state for-profit child care operator*

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY — THE FIVE THINGS THAT MATTER

1. **Licensing sits at the Department of Education, not DHS.** Confirmed current. This is a genuinely different regulator personality than most states — an education agency, outcome/kindergarten-readiness oriented, running licensing.
2. **The subsidy program has been renamed and re-platformed** — "Child Care Assistance Program" is now **School Readiness Assistance (SRA)**, on a separate portal. Rates and copays were restructured effective Oct 1 / Nov 1, 2025 under fiscal duress.
3. **A funding cliff was papered over, not solved.** The October–November 2025 "sustainability" package runs **through September 30, 2026** — i.e., it expires *within weeks of this report's date*. This is the single most urgent item.
4. **ABC pre-K is stagnant and the legislature just refused to fix it** (April 22, 2026). ABC pays ~$28/day vs. $33–36 elsewhere, with no significant increase since 2008.
5. **No employer child care tax credit passed in 2025** — despite widespread third-party claims to the contrary. See the correction flag in §5.

## 1. LICENSING & REGULATION

### Regulating agency — VERIFIED

Child care licensing in Arkansas is administered by the **Arkansas Department of Education, Division of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE), Office of Early Childhood (OEC)**. This is confirmed by the federal ACF licensing database, which lists the licensing contact as exactly that entity ([ACF Licensing Regulations Database — Arkansas](https://licensingregulations.acf.hhs.gov/licensing/states-territories/arkansas); [ACF contact record](https://licensingregulations.acf.hhs.gov/licensing/contact/arkansas-department-education-division-elementary-and-secondary-education-office)), and by DESE's own licensing page ([DESE OEC — Child Care Licensing](https://dese.ade.arkansas.gov/Offices/office-of-early-childhood/child-care-licensing)).

**The transfer:** The **2023 LEARNS Act**, following Gov. Sanders's **January 11, 2023 executive order**, moved the Office of Early Childhood from the **Department of Human Services** (where it was the Division of Child Care and Early Childhood Education, "DCCECE") to the **Department of Education** ([Arkansas Advocate, Oct. 29, 2025](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2025/10/29/report-recommends-steps-to-improve-arkansas-child-care-licensing/); [Arkansas Advocate, provider database brief](https://arkansasadvocate.com/briefs/arkansas-department-of-education-creates-searchable-child-care-provider-database/); [ADE, *An Analysis of Arkansas's Early Childhood System*](https://learns.ade.arkansas.gov/File/0l1xkwt1.pdf)).

> **Operator alert — stale-artifact risk.** Legacy DHS pages remain live ([portal.arkansas.gov DCCECE](https://portal.arkansas.gov/state_agencies/department-of-human-services-dhs/division-of-child-care-and-early-childhood-education/); [humanservices.arkansas.gov licensing](http://humanservices.arkansas.gov/dccece/Pages/ChildCareLicensing.aspx)). Worse, **DESE's own Child Care Assistance page still instructs applicants to mail DHS at "P.O. Box 1437, Slot S145"** and links a **2021** CCDF sliding fee chart ([DESE — Child Care Assistance Program](https://dese.ade.arkansas.gov/Offices/office-of-early-childhood/child-care-assistance-program)). Do not let center directors work from these. Route all compliance questions to OEC and the SRA portal.

### OEC structure

Four units: **Child Care Licensing and Regulatory Checks; Direct Services and Operations; Content and Development; Local Leads** ([DESE — About OEC](https://dese.ade.arkansas.gov/Offices/office-of-early-childhood/about-oec)).

### Facility categories

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| Registered Child Care Family Home | Fewer than 5 children |
| Licensed Child Care Family Home | 6–16 children; additional approval required at 11+ |
| Licensed Child Care Center | 6+ children from multiple families; exempt if operating <10 hrs/week |
| Out-of-School-Time Facility | Center-based, school-age only |

Source: [DESE — Child Care Licensing](https://dese.ade.arkansas.gov/Offices/office-of-early-childhood/child-care-licensing). Statutory authority: **Childcare Facility Licensing Act, Ark. Code § 20-78-201 to § 20-78-230** ([statute compilation hosted by DESE](https://dese.ade.arkansas.gov/Files/Arkansas_Statutes_&_Court_Rules___Subchapter_2%E2%80%94Childcare_Facility_Licensing_Act_(%C2%A7_20%E2%80%9378%E2%80%93201_to_%C2%A7_20%E2%80%9378%E2%80%93230)___Westlaw_Advantage_OEC.pdf)).

### Ratios and group size — centers

Per the **Minimum Licensing Requirements for Child Care Centers** ([full text, ACF-hosted](https://licensingregulations.acf.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/licensing_regulation/AR_Center_Dec_2020_508.pdf); codified at [016.22.20-005](https://regulations.justia.com/states/arkansas/agency-016/division-22/rule-016-22-20-005/)):

| Age | Staff:Child | Max group size |
|---|---|---|
| Birth – 18 months | 1:5 | 10 |
| 18 – 36 months | 1:8 | 16 |
| 2.5 – 3 years | 1:12 | 24 |
| 4 years | 1:15 | 30 |
| 5 years – kindergarten | 1:18 | 36 |
| Kindergarten and above | 1:18 | 36 |

**Group size rule:** "Group size shall be limited to two (2) times the number of children allowed with one (1) staff member." Programs of ≤8 children may mix ages, but the caregiver must meet the ratio for the **youngest** child present.

**Competitive read:** These infant and toddler ratios (1:5, 1:8) are **more permissive than the national median** and materially better for unit economics than neighboring states with 1:4 infant rules. The 1:15 four-year-old ratio is also on the loose end. This is a labor-cost advantage worth protecting — and worth watching, because any "quality" push in 2027 will target exactly these numbers.

### Staff qualifications

**Directors** — minimum age 21, and one of:
- BA in early childhood/child development or related field; or
- BA in unrelated field + 4 years ECE experience (or CDA/Birth–Pre-K credential); or
- AA in early childhood + 6 years experience; or
- 8 years experience + professional credential obtained within 2 years.

Directors must be **on-site at least 50% of the center's primary operational day on a routine basis** — a real constraint for multi-site operators using roving/shared leadership models.

**Staff** — minimum age 18 (16–17 permitted only under direct supervision); high school diploma or GED required for all staff working directly with children.

### Training

- Directors / assistant directors: **15 clock hours/year** in ECE.
- Direct care staff: **15 hours/year** continuing ECE.
- New staff: **8 hours orientation + 15 hours job-specific training within 3 months**.

Source: [MLR for Child Care Centers](https://licensingregulations.acf.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/licensing_regulation/AR_Center_Dec_2020_508.pdf).

### Background checks

| Check | Scope | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| FBI criminal records | Owners, operators, direct care staff, anyone with supervisory/disciplinary control | Initial + **every 5 years** |
| Arkansas State Police (incl. fingerprinting + sex offender registry) | Same | Initial + **every 5 years** |
| Child Maltreatment Registry | Same | Prior to employment + **every 2 years** |

Certain felonies are **permanent** bars; others are waivable after 5–10 years with no subsequent convictions.

### Inspection & enforcement

The MLR specifies inspection "on a routine basis," with **unscheduled visits** when violations are cited, and **annual license renewal** requiring compliance verification. The regulation as published does not commit to a numeric annual inspection frequency — **UNCONFIRMED** whether OEC has adopted a published risk-based inspection schedule post-transfer.

**Enforcement leverage via subsidy:** The CCDF/SRA Participant Agreement provides **immediate termination** of subsidy participation upon license revocation, **probationary or provisional license status**, loss of Better Beginnings Level 2, or federal exclusion-list placement — with a three-stage non-compliance notice process (15-day response windows) that can escalate to **billing holds** ([CCDF Program Participant Agreement, eff. 11/03/2025](https://sra.ade.arkansas.gov/Resources/2af404e5-b20d-49c7-8e05-29101bced34c?handler=Download)). **Note this carefully:** a *provisional* license — not just revocation — cuts off subsidy revenue. In a state where subsidy is a meaningful revenue line, that is a severe cliff and a strong argument for over-investing in licensing compliance.

### Rule revisions 2025–2026

The operative center MLR edition available through the federal database remains the **December 2020** edition. **UNCONFIRMED** whether OEC has promulgated a comprehensive post-transfer MLR rewrite. The **Arkansas Early Childhood Commission** holds statutory authority to "review and approve proposed rules for child care licensing standards" ([DESE — AECC](https://dese.ade.arkansas.gov/Offices/office-of-early-childhood/arkansas-early-childhood-commission)); its published minutes archive runs only through **April 2025 (posted July 29, 2025)**, which limits visibility. **This is the single largest verification gap in this report** — see §9.

### The licensing-reform report (October–November 2025)

**United WE** commissioned the **University of Arkansas Office for Education Policy (OEP)** for a year-long study, with additional **Walton Family Foundation** funding; released **October 29, 2025**, with the policy brief (Vol. 22, Issue 7) published **November 5, 2025** ([Arkansas Advocate](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2025/10/29/report-recommends-steps-to-improve-arkansas-child-care-licensing/); [OEP announcement](https://oep.uark.edu/breaking-barriers-to-child-care-licensing-in-arkansas/); [full brief PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wordpressua.uark.edu/dist/1/555/files/2025/11/ECE-Licensing-Policy-Brief2.pdf)).

**Method:** 32 providers in focus groups; 50+ expert/stakeholder interviews. **Provider satisfaction with licensing averaged 5.7/10.** Providers reported extensive documentation, **overlapping inspections**, and long approval periods.

**Six recommendations:** streamline licensing and align state/local requirements; financial support (subsidies, insurance options, facility-upgrade grants); workforce development and career pathways; **modernize zoning to permit child care in residential areas and establish "Child Care Zones"**; diversify funding beyond federal dependence; recruit family child care providers.

Sarah McKenzie, OEP executive director: *"Streamlining licensing and inspections is the easiest barrier to remove."* Sen. **Breanne Davis (R-Russellville)** — now Senate Majority Whip — publicly engaged on women's underemployment tied to child care access. **She is your highest-value legislative relationship in this space.**

## 2. SUBSIDY / CCDF — SCHOOL READINESS ASSISTANCE (SRA)

### Rebranding and platform

The Child Care Assistance Program is now **School Readiness Assistance**, on a dedicated portal at [sra.ade.arkansas.gov](https://sra.ade.arkansas.gov/), launched **November 18, 2024** with Citizen, Worker, and Provider portals (application management, digital locker, attendance tracking) ([SRA Newsroom](https://sra.ade.arkansas.gov/Newsroom)).

### Rate methodology — VERIFIED

Rates are set from the **Arkansas Market Rate Survey at the 75th percentile of costs, with a standardized rate by county** ([CCDF Participant Agreement, eff. 11/03/2025](https://sra.ade.arkansas.gov/Resources/2af404e5-b20d-49c7-8e05-29101bced34c?handler=Download)).

A **statewide market rate** applies, **except Benton and Washington counties (Northwest Arkansas), which carry a higher market rate** reflecting higher cost of living ([ADE press release, Sept. 19, 2025](https://sra.ade.arkansas.gov/Newsroom/Details?id=ec18c6d9-553e-4d9b-9025-8a01d4d88799); [Arkansas Advocate, Oct. 30, 2025](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2025/10/30/arkansas-department-of-education-will-decide-whether-to-accept-child-care-sustainability-proposal/)).

**Known rate data point:** a family of three with a child under 4 and income below **$30,334** qualifies for **up to $35/day** in assistance ([ADE, Sept. 19, 2025](https://sra.ade.arkansas.gov/Newsroom/Details?id=ec18c6d9-553e-4d9b-9025-8a01d4d88799)). Separately, in April 2026 testimony, ABC was described as paying **$28/day versus $33–$36/day for other early childhood programs** — the $33–36 band is the best available proxy for current SRA daily rates ([Arkansas Advocate, Apr. 22, 2026](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2026/04/22/arkansas-oks-proposal-aimed-at-assisting-newly-formed-smaller-school-districts/)).

**The complete county-by-county, age-by-age, Better Beginnings-level rate table is not publicly posted** on the SRA Resources page ([SRA Resources](https://sra.ade.arkansas.gov/Resources)). **Action item: obtain via direct request to OEC or through your provider portal login.**

### Tiered rates by quality level

The approved restructuring **consolidates payment tiers so that rates are identical for Better Beginnings levels 3–4 and identical for levels 5–6** — designed to avoid direct payment cuts while preserving quality incentives ([Arkansas Advocate, Oct. 30, 2025](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2025/10/30/arkansas-department-of-education-will-decide-whether-to-accept-child-care-sustainability-proposal/)).

### Enrollment vs. attendance payment — MIXED / PARTIAL COMPLIANCE

The Participant Agreement states OEC "agrees to reimburse the Participant for **services delivered**" — attendance-anchored language, not the enrollment-based payment the 2024 federal rule pushes. Arkansas softens this with a **generous absence allowance** rather than true enrollment-based payment:

| Period | Monthly cap | Period cap |
|---|---|---|
| July–October | 6 days | 12 days |
| November–February | 8 days | 16 days |
| March–June | 6 days | 12 days |
| **Annual ceiling** | — | **40 days** (without written waiver) |

Plus: **up to 5 paid professional-development days per year** (advance agenda submission required), and **holiday billing permitted** when the facility is closed and the child was scheduled.

**Billing deadline — material operational risk:** "OEC is not obligated to pay bills more than **thirty (30) calendar days after the date of service**" absent prior written approval. Late submission = forfeited revenue. Verify your Arkansas billing cycle clears this comfortably.

Source: [CCDF Program Participant Agreement](https://sra.ade.arkansas.gov/Resources/2af404e5-b20d-49c7-8e05-29101bced34c?handler=Download).

### Eligibility thresholds — VERIFIED

Monthly income limits, **effective October 1, 2025** ([AR SMI Monthly Income Chart](https://sra.ade.arkansas.gov/Resources/c52d73c4-baeb-4665-9adc-29f640d4b156?handler=Download)):

| Family size | 40% SMI | 60% SMI | 85% SMI | 100% SMI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $1,564.85 | $2,347.28 | $3,325.31 | $3,912.14 |
| 2 | $2,046.35 | $3,069.52 | $4,348.49 | $5,115.88 |
| 3 | $2,527.84 | $3,791.76 | $5,371.66 | $6,319.61 |
| 4 | $3,009.33 | $4,514.00 | $6,394.83 | $7,523.34 |
| 5 | $3,490.83 | $5,236.24 | $7,418.01 | $8,727.08 |

Conversion multipliers: weekly × 4.334; bi-weekly × 2.167.

Other criteria: children **birth through age 12**; family assets **under $1 million**; parents working or in school/training. Priority to children with special needs, children experiencing homelessness, and TANF families. Protective-services children qualify separately ([OEP analysis](https://oep.uark.edu/early-child-care-part-1-of-2-whats-behind-arkansass-school-readiness-assistance-child-care-subsidies/)).

**Work requirement raised from 20 to 30 hours per week** — adopted November 2025 ([Arkansas Advocate, Nov. 3, 2025](https://arkansasadvocate.com/briefs/arkansas-education-department-adopts-most-of-plan-to-keep-families-providers-in-child-care-industry/)). **This is a demand-side headwind:** it will disqualify part-time-working parents and likely shrink your subsidy-funded census at the margin.

**72-month lifetime limit eliminated** — a demand-side tailwind partially offsetting the above.

### Copays and the federal 7% cap — PARTIALLY RESOLVED

- **Zero copay** for families at or below the state poverty level (working or in school).
- **Sliding scale** above that. Example: family of three just under **$64,459** annually pays up to **$12/day** copay (age-dependent).
- **Uniform copayment percentages** across age groups for families with multiple children — **ADOPTED**.
- **Capping multi-child family copays at 7% of household income** and **eliminating copays below 60% SMI** — **STILL UNDER CONSIDERATION** as of November 3, 2025, at an estimated cost of **$5–7 million**.

Sources: [ADE Sept. 19, 2025](https://sra.ade.arkansas.gov/Newsroom/Details?id=ec18c6d9-553e-4d9b-9025-8a01d4d88799); [Arkansas Advocate Oct. 30](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2025/10/30/arkansas-department-of-education-will-decide-whether-to-accept-child-care-sustainability-proposal/) and [Nov. 3, 2025](https://arkansasadvocate.com/briefs/arkansas-education-department-adopts-most-of-plan-to-keep-families-providers-in-child-care-industry/).

> **UNCONFIRMED — HIGH PRIORITY:** whether Arkansas adopted the 7% cap between November 2025 and August 2026. This is a **federal CCDF requirement**, not optional, and Arkansas's hedging suggests a compliance gap. Resolve directly with OEC.

## 3. STATE PRE-K — ARKANSAS BETTER CHANCE (ABC)

### Program structure

Established **1991**; expanded in **2003** to reach low-income preschoolers in academically at-risk areas. Serves **birth to five** with developmental and socio-economic risk factors. Three delivery models: **center-based, home-visiting (HIPPY and PAT), and family child care homes** ([DESE — ABC](https://dese.ade.arkansas.gov/Offices/office-of-early-childhood/arkansas-better-chance-program-abc)).

**Private and for-profit centers are eligible.** The 2025–26 finance guidance explicitly distinguishes "LEAs (school districts, coops, universities)" from **"private programs"** with different administrative cost caps — direct confirmation that non-LEA providers participate ([ABC 2025–2026 Finance Training](https://dese.ade.arkansas.gov/Files/2025_-_2026_Finance_Training_Slides_OEC.pdf)).

> **Caveat for pursuit modeling:** Rep. Mayberry stated in April 2026 that **"most ABC providers operate within public school systems"** — she argued 75% of any appropriation increase would flow back to public schools ([Arkansas Advocate, Apr. 22, 2026](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2026/04/22/arkansas-oks-proposal-aimed-at-assisting-newly-formed-smaller-school-districts/)). **The private/for-profit share of ABC slots is UNCONFIRMED and appears to be a minority.** Obtain the provider-type breakdown from OEC before sizing this opportunity.

### 2025–26 funding rates — VERIFIED

| Model | Per child / year | Per child / month |
|---|---|---|
| **Center-based** | **$5,105.00** | $510.50 |
| **Home-visiting** | **$1,894.00** | $189.40 |

**Terms:**
- **40% match required** (ABC reimburses 60%). Match may be **cash or in-kind** (donated goods, services, equipment, utilities, labor). Match sources include school district, agency, Title I, ESA, ARPA, CARES funding.
- **Administrative cost caps: LEAs 12%; private programs 15%.**
- Payment **monthly by the 5th, October through April**, based on enrollment entered in **COPA**.
- **Separate bank account required** for ABC funds — no commingling.

Source: [ABC 2025–2026 Finance Training Slides](https://dese.ade.arkansas.gov/Files/2025_-_2026_Finance_Training_Slides_OEC.pdf).

> **Economics warning:** At **$5,105/child/year center-based with a 40% match obligation and a 15% admin cap**, ABC is a **thin-margin-to-loss-leader** program for a for-profit operator absent a strong in-kind match strategy. The in-kind flexibility (utilities, labor, equipment) is the key lever. Model carefully before expanding ABC classrooms.

### Statewide funding and enrollment (2024–25, NIEER)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|

| State spending | **$106,072,095** |
| TANF supplement | **$7,500,000** |

**Note the tension:** the requested "significant increase in recent budgets" shows up as a **9% inflation-adjusted bump in total dollars with essentially flat enrollment (+16 children)** — i.e., the increase went to per-child adequacy and to backfilling expiring federal recovery funds, **not to slot expansion**. And note that **$14.0M of the total was one-time federal recovery money** now expiring — a second cliff embedded in the ABC number.

### ABC quality standards

- Staff-to-child ratio **1:10**; maximum class size **20** for 3- and 4-year-olds.
- **One bachelor's degree holder per three classrooms**; associate degree holders in remaining classrooms.
- Comprehensive health and developmental screenings; structured classroom observations.

Source: [NIEER](https://nieer.org/yearbook/2025/state-profiles/arkansas). **ABC's 1:10 ratio and 1-BA-per-3-classrooms rule are materially stricter than baseline licensing (1:15 at age 4, no degree requirement)** — factor the staffing delta into any ABC classroom pro forma.

### Act 504 of 2025 — ABC / ABCSS consolidation — VERIFIED

**Act 504 (HB1733)**, sponsored by **Reps. Joey Carr and Tosh** and **Sen. Caldwell**, **approved April 10, 2025**, consolidates the **Arkansas Better Chance Program** and the **Arkansas Better Chance for School Success Program** "to promote efficiency," across approximately **23,000 available preschool slots**.

Mechanism: DESE "shall establish **the same criteria and qualifications for eligibility**" for both programs. The act **repealed the prior section defining eligibility at 200% of federal poverty guidelines**, letting DESE set unified eligibility based on 2024–25 school-year standards. Programs may now serve **birth–5** using ABC Program Guide Section 6 criteria — family income, **parental education level, low birth weight, limited English proficiency, developmental delays**, and other family circumstances.

Sources: [Act 504 text](https://www.arkleg.state.ar.us/Acts/FTPDocument?path=%2FACTS%2F2025R%2FPublic%2F&file=504.pdf&ddBienniumSession=2025%2F2025R); [ABC provider call slides](https://dese.ade.arkansas.gov/Files/ABC_April_Slides_OEC.pdf); [NIEER](https://nieer.org/yearbook/2025/state-profiles/arkansas).

**Strategic read:** Act 504 **broadens** the eligible child population beyond a pure income test. For a for-profit operator, this expands the addressable ABC-eligible census in mixed-income centers — the risk-factor criteria (parental education, LBW, LEP) reach families well above 200% FPL.

### The April 2026 rate fight — LOST

**April 22, 2026:** **Rep. Julie Mayberry (R-Hensley)** brought a proposal to the **Joint Budget Committee's Special Language Subcommittee** to appropriate **~$22 million** from the state's **public school adequacy fund** and require **ABC providers be reimbursed at no less than 75% of current market rates**.

**It was rejected.**

Mayberry's case: ABC pays **$28/day** vs. **$33–$36/day** for other early childhood programs; **"ABC programs have not had a significant increase in funding since 2008"**; 70% of participating pre-K students reached kindergarten readiness; 75% of the appropriation would return to public schools.

**Sen. Jonathan Dismang** objected to drawing from the public school adequacy fund given concerns that **Educational Freedom Accounts (vouchers)** are already straining public education.

Source: [Arkansas Advocate, Apr. 22, 2026](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2026/04/22/arkansas-oks-proposal-aimed-at-assisting-newly-formed-smaller-school-districts/).

**Read this correctly:** the fight was lost **not on the merits of child care** but on the **funding source**. ABC rate adequacy is now entangled with the voucher-vs-public-schools budget war. **A 2027 ABC increase needs a funding source outside the adequacy fund** — general revenue, TANF, or surplus. That is the coalition design problem to solve before January 2027.

## 4. QUALITY RATING SYSTEM — BETTER BEGINNINGS

### Structure — a scale in transition

Better Beginnings is Arkansas's **voluntary** quality rating and improvement system, providing "tools, guidance, and training" to licensed providers ([DESE — About OEC](https://dese.ade.arkansas.gov/Offices/office-of-early-childhood/about-oec)). It rates **child care centers, child care family homes, and out-of-school-time programs** ([arbetterbeginnings.com](https://arbetterbeginnings.com/)).

**The scale is now 1–6, not 1–3.** The Childcare Arkansas public database lets parents filter by **"Better Beginnings quality ratings (1-6 scale)"** ([Arkansas Advocate, provider database](https://arkansasadvocate.com/briefs/arkansas-department-of-education-creates-searchable-child-care-provider-database/)), and the 2025 rate restructuring explicitly references **"quality levels 3-4 and 5-6"** ([Arkansas Advocate, Oct. 30, 2025](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2025/10/30/arkansas-department-of-education-will-decide-whether-to-accept-child-care-sustainability-proposal/)).

> **Documentation lag:** the public Better Beginnings site still publishes only **"Level One / Two / Three Star Kits"** ([arbetterbeginnings.com/providers](https://arbetterbeginnings.com/providers/)) — legacy 3-star artifacts. **The published requirements for levels 4, 5, and 6 are UNCONFIRMED and not publicly documented.** Request the current level criteria from OEC directly; this is a material gap for any operator planning a rating-advancement strategy.

### Tie to subsidy rates — VERIFIED AND CONSEQUENTIAL

Two hard linkages:

1. **Floor:** Loss of **Better Beginnings Level 2** triggers **immediate termination** from CCDF/SRA participation ([CCDF Participant Agreement](https://sra.ade.arkansas.gov/Resources/2af404e5-b20d-49c7-8e05-29101bced34c?handler=Download)). **Level 2 is the price of admission to subsidy revenue.**
2. **Tiering:** Reimbursement is tiered by level, with the 2025 restructuring **collapsing 3–4 into one rate and 5–6 into one rate** ([Arkansas Advocate, Oct. 30, 2025](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2025/10/30/arkansas-department-of-education-will-decide-whether-to-accept-child-care-sustainability-proposal/)).

**Operator implication:** the tier collapse **destroys the marginal ROI of moving from 3→4 and from 5→6.** The only rate-relevant moves are **2→3** and **4→5**. Re-sequence any quality-investment plan accordingly. Also note the 2025 changes affected **"quality incentive payments"** ([OEP](https://oep.uark.edu/early-child-care-part-1-of-2-whats-behind-arkansass-school-readiness-assistance-child-care-subsidies/)) — confirm current incentive amounts.

### Historical quality distribution

The 2023 LEARNS-era system analysis reported a distribution of **34% three-star, 38% two-star, 10% one-star, 18% unrated** ([ADE, *Analysis of Arkansas's Early Childhood System*](https://learns.ade.arkansas.gov/File/0l1xkwt1.pdf)). *(That document also cites "7,211 licensed providers," a figure that appears inconsistent with other Arkansas provider counts and should be treated as **UNRELIABLE** pending verification — it may count classrooms, sites, or individuals rather than facilities.)*

### Childcare Arkansas database — launched July 8, 2025

Parent-facing searchable database mandated by the LEARNS Act. Filters: county/city/ZIP, Better Beginnings rating (1–6), ages accepted, hours of operation, transportation, **LEARNS school voucher acceptance**, interactive map. Also supports **filing complaints against facilities** and links to the SRA portal.

Gov. Sanders: *"Through the LEARNS Act…we are empowering Arkansas families to make informed decisions."*

Source: [Arkansas Advocate](https://arkansasadvocate.com/briefs/arkansas-department-of-education-creates-searchable-child-care-provider-database/).

> **Reputational exposure:** your Arkansas centers' ratings, hours, and **complaint history** are now consumer-facing and map-searchable. Audit your listings for accuracy — errors in a state-run parent-choice tool are a direct enrollment risk.

### Compliance burden — the operator's summary

Cost advantages: **permissive ratios** (1:5 infant, 1:8 toddler, 1:15 four-year-old); no degree requirement for classroom staff (HS/GED only); relatively generous subsidy absence allowances (40 days/year plus 5 PD days plus holidays).
