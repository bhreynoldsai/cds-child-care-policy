## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY — THE FIVE THINGS THAT MATTER

1. **The 2024 CCDF rule has been substantially rolled back.** HHS/ACF's final rule *"Restoring Flexibility in the Child Care and Development Fund"* (91 FR 25796, May 12, 2026, RIN 0970-AD20, **effective July 13, 2026**) rescinded the four provider-facing mandates: the 7% family copay cap, enrollment-based payment, prospective payment, and the grants/contracts requirement. States *may* keep them; they are no longer *required* to. Revenue predictability in your subsidy book now becomes a **state-by-state** question, not a federal floor. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/12/2026-09382/restoring-flexibility-in-the-child-care-and-development-fund); [NAEYC](https://www.naeyc.org/our-work/public-policy-advocacy/2026-ccdf-final-rule))

2. **Federal funding is flat-to-nominal.** FY2026 enacted (signed Feb 3, 2026): CCDBG **$8.831B** (+$85M), Head Start **$12.357B** (+$85M), PDG B-5 **$315M**. FY2027 House mark: CCDBG $8.841B, Head Start $12.367B (+$10M each), **PDG B-5 eliminated**. ([CLASP](https://www.clasp.org/publications/fact-sheet/fy26-funding-for-ccdbg-offers-important-increase-amidst-challenging-times-for-children-and-families/); [FFYF](https://www.ffyf.org/2026/02/04/statement-fy2026-funding-bill-boosts-federal-investment-in-child-care-and-early-learning/); [CCAoA](https://info.childcareaware.org/blog/protecting-early-childhood-investments-fy27))

3. **The tax side is the genuine growth story.** The 2025 reconciliation law (enacted July 4, 2025) tripled the Section 45F employer child care credit cap to $500K/$600K and raised the rate to 40%/50%, **effective tax years beginning after Dec 31, 2025** — and for the first time allows **jointly owned/pooled multi-employer facilities** to qualify. DCAP exclusion rose to $7,500. This is the strongest federal tailwind for an employer-partnership line in two decades. ([BPC](https://bipartisanpolicy.org/explainer/45f-employer-provided-child-care-tax-credit-2026-guide/); [Mercer](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/insights/us-health-news/big-beautiful-bill-permanently-enhances-dependent-care-benefits/))

4. **CCDBG reauthorization is live but not moving.** S. 2828 / H.R. 9224 (Child Care Modernization Act) are bipartisan and bicameral, hearing held March 19, 2026 — **no markup in either chamber**. ([govinfo bill status](https://www.govinfo.gov/bulkdata/BILLSTATUS/119/s/BILLSTATUS-119s2828.xml))

5. **Head Start's 2024 wage/benefits mandates are proposed for rescission but NOT yet final.** The NPRM (91 FR 25842, May 12, 2026) closed comments June 11, 2026. As of Aug 3, 2026 no final rule has published. Until then, the Aug 1, 2028 (benefits) and Aug 1, 2031 (wages) deadlines remain technically on the books. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/12/2026-09383/restoring-flexibility-to-support-head-start-program-access))

## 2. THE CCDF RULES — 2024 MANDATES AND THE 2026 ROLLBACK

### 2.1 The 2024 final rule (the baseline you have been building toward)
**"Improving Child Care Access, Affordability, and Stability in the CCDF"** — **89 FR 15366**, published **March 1, 2024**, RIN **0970-AD02**, **effective April 30, 2024**. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/03/01/2024-04139/improving-child-care-access-affordability-and-stability-in-the-child-care-and-development-fund-ccdf))

Provider-facing mandates it imposed:
- **Family copayments capped at 7% of family income.**
- **Enrollment-based payment** rather than attendance.
- **Prospective payment** (pay at the start of the service period, as private-pay families are billed).
- **Grants and contracts** required for some direct services — targeted at underserved areas, infants/toddlers, and children with disabilities.
- **Full payment rate permitted** even where the state rate exceeds the provider's private-pay price — a meaningful margin provision.

**Compliance mechanics:** demonstrated through the **FFY2025–2027 CCDF Plans, due July 1, 2024, effective Oct 1, 2024**; Tribal lead agencies via FFY2026–2028 Plans effective Oct 1, 2025. States unable to comply could request **temporary waivers of up to two years**.

**Waiver timeline — critical:** waivers initially ran through **August 1, 2026**. In **late August 2025**, ACF **extended additional waiver requests through August 1, 2028**. ([New America, Oct 23, 2025](https://www.newamerica.org/insights/with-ccdf-rule-in-limbo-next-steps-for-states-are-uncertain/); see also [ACF-OCC-CCDF-PI-24-03](https://acf.gov/occ/policy-guidance/2024-ccdf-final-rule-transitional-waiver-flexibilities-acf-occ-ccdf-pi-24-03) — *full text not retrievable, ACF returned 403*)

### 2.2 The 2026 rescission — what actually changed and when
**Sequence:**
- **NPRM: January 5, 2026** — *"Restoring Flexibility in the Child Care and Development Fund (CCDF),"* doc 2025-24272, 91 FR 207–215. ([Justia mirror](https://regulations.justia.com/regulations/fedreg/2026/01/05/2025-24272.html))
- **Final rule: May 12, 2026** — **91 FR 25796**, doc **2026-09382**, RIN **0970-AD20**. **Effective July 13, 2026.** ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/12/2026-09382/restoring-flexibility-in-the-child-care-and-development-fund))

| Policy | 2024 rule | **2026 rule (in effect July 13, 2026)** |
|---|---|---|
| Family copay | Hard cap at 7% of income | **Cap rescinded.** States set their own sliding scale; must only ensure copays are "not a barrier to families." |
| Payment basis | Enrollment-based required | **Mandate eliminated.** States may choose: (a) enrollment-based, (b) 85% attendance threshold, (c) five-or-fewer absences/month, or (d) an alternative justified in the state plan. |
| Payment timing | Prospective required | **Mandate removed.** States may pay prospectively **or** within **21 calendar days of invoice receipt**. |
| Supply-building | Grants/contracts required for underserved areas, infants/toddlers, children with disabilities | **Requirement eliminated.** States retain discretion; parental certificate option preserved. |
| Payment practices | — | **Retained:** lead agencies must still provide providers **written payment agreements** specifying rates, payment schedules, and **dispute resolution** processes. |

**States may still adopt any of these.** The rule preamble states lead agencies "continue to have the flexibility to implement the policies formerly required by the March 2024 final rule."

**Baseline state adoption at the time of rescission** (two sources, differing counts — use the range):
- 7% copay cap: **31 states + DC** at ≤7%; 16 states and 5 territories below 7% (per the final rule); NAEYC says "nearly all states," with Alaska, Maine, and Minnesota having recently legislated compliance.
- Enrollment-based payment: **~29 lead agencies** (final rule) / **25+ states** (NAEYC), including ME, MI, SC, OH, WV.
- Prospective payment: **9 lead agencies** (final rule) / **12+** (NAEYC).
- Grants/contracts: **10 states** (NAEYC); Maine legislated a requirement.

**Backsliding already observed:** **Missouri paused prospective payments**; **Washington eliminated funding for enrollment-based payment initiatives.** ([NAEYC](https://www.naeyc.org/our-work/public-policy-advocacy/2026-ccdf-final-rule))

**Rulemaking record:** **1,244 comments** (1,138 posted; 998 unique). 787 individual submissions; **276 from child care providers**; **only 7 state CCDF agencies** commented. The majority of unique comments **opposed** the rescissions; **most state commenters supported** them. That split is the single most useful fact for your state-level strategy: state administrators wanted the flexibility back.

**Regulatory impact analysis:** annualized transfers $23.4M (3% discount) / $22.8M (7%); annualized cost savings $6.7M (3%) / $6.6M (7%); $6.1M/year in perpetuity at 7%.

### 2.3 Compliance dates falling in 2026–2027 — what to watch
- **July 13, 2026 — effective date. Already passed.** The federal floor is gone as of three weeks ago.
- **Forthcoming ACF guidance on terminating existing transitional and legislative waivers.** The final rule expressly states: *"Forthcoming guidance will outline the process HHS will follow to terminate existing transitional and legislative waivers for rescinded requirements."* **This guidance has not been located as of Aug 3, 2026 — treat as pending and high-priority to monitor.** It is the operative document determining whether states that took waivers through Aug 1, 2028 simply have those waivers voided, and how quickly.
- **The rule sets no new state compliance deadlines.** Absence of a deadline means change happens on each state's own legislative/plan cycle — which is why this is now an 11-state monitoring problem rather than a single federal date.
- **Next CCDF State Plan cycle:** FFY2028–2030 Plans. By the pattern of the FFY2025–2027 cycle (due July 1, 2024, effective Oct 1, 2024), the FFY2028–2030 Plans would be **due ~July 1, 2027, effective Oct 1, 2027**. **UNCONFIRMED** — ACF has not published the FFY2028–2030 preprint/deadline in any source located.

### 2.5 Practical read for a 260-center operator
- **The margin risks are:** (a) reversion to attendance-based payment in states where you have high subsidy census and normal absenteeism — direct revenue loss; (b) reversion to arrears payment (up to 21 days post-invoice) — working capital hit; (c) copays above 7% — collection risk and family attrition pushed onto you.
- **The retained protection worth invoking:** written payment agreements specifying **rates, schedules, and dispute resolution** remain federally required. Where a state moves to attendance-based or arrears payment, that agreement is your contractual anchor.
- **The asymmetry:** states that already built enrollment-based and prospective payment systems have sunk costs and generally will not reverse absent budget pressure. States under fiscal stress (MO, WA are the demonstrated cases) will. Prioritize monitoring by state fiscal condition, not by political lean.

### 3.2 FY2027 — the restructuring proposal is the real story
The **April 2026 President's Budget** requests **flat funding at $12.357B** but proposes **allowing state child care licensing standards to substitute for the Head Start Program Performance Standards**, with budget language asserting this would *"allow the program to serve more children with the same level of funding."* NAEYC's objection: most state ratio requirements are weaker than Head Start's. ([NAEYC](https://www.naeyc.org/our-work/public-policy-advocacy/2027-president-budget-ece))

**Strategic implication for you:** if HSPPS were replaced by state licensing standards, the cost differential between a Head Start slot and a licensed private slot narrows sharply — which cuts both ways. It reduces the regulatory moat around Head Start grantees and makes partnership/delegate arrangements cheaper to operate, but it also makes Head Start grantees more directly competitive with private centers on cost per slot.

### 3.3 The 2024 Performance Standards rule — staff wage provisions
**"Supporting the Head Start Workforce and Consistent Quality Programming,"** published **August 21, 2024**, doc 2024-18279, RIN **0970-AC63**. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/21/2024-18279/supporting-the-head-start-workforce-and-consistent-quality-programming))

Compliance dates as codified:
- **§1302.90(e) — wages: compliance by August 1, 2031.** Requires pay scales for all staff; education-staff wage comparability with public preschool teachers (or 90% of public kindergarten teacher pay); salaries sufficient to cover basic living costs in the geographic area; wage comparability across preschool and infant/toddler (EHS) programs.
- **§1302.90(f) — benefits: compliance by August 1, 2028.** Health coverage for full-time staff; paid leave; behavioral health access; facilitation of child care subsidies and student loan forgiveness; five-year benefits reassessment cycle.

Flexibilities added in the final rule versus the Dec 2023 NPRM: a **waiver if FY2028 appropriations increases fall below 1.3%**; modified/phased requirements for programs with **≤200 funded slots**; benefits phased over four years rather than two; no paid FMLA obligation beyond existing law. ([AASA summary](https://www.aasa.org/resources/blog/hhs-releases-head-start-final-rule-with-more-flexibility-on-wage-requirements))

A **technical corrections notice published October 31, 2024** clarified implementation dates. ([HeadStart.gov Performance Standards Information Center](https://headstart.gov/policy/article/performance-standards-information-center))

## 4. FEDERAL TAX PROVISIONS — THE EMPLOYER-PARTNERSHIP OPPORTUNITY

All three provisions were amended by the **2025 reconciliation law ("One Big Beautiful Bill Act"), enacted July 4, 2025.**

### 4.1 Section 45F — Employer-Provided Child Care Credit (the big one)

| | Prior law | **Post-2025 law (TY2026+)** |
|---|---|---|
| Credit rate, general | 25% of qualified child care expenditures | **40%** |
| Credit rate, eligible small business | 25% | **50%** |
| Resource & referral rate | 10% | 10% |
| Annual cap, general | $150,000 | **$500,000** (inflation-indexed) |
| Annual cap, small business | $150,000 | **$600,000** (inflation-indexed) |
| Small business threshold | n/a | gross receipts under **~$32M** |
| Pooled/joint facilities | Not permitted | **Businesses that contract to jointly own, fund, and/or operate a facility with other businesses now qualify** |

- Qualifying expenditures include facility construction/renovation, operating expenses, **third-party provider contracts**, and resource & referral fees.
- Applies to up to **$1.2M of qualified child care operations expenditures** and up to **$1.5M of qualifying resource and referral expenditures**.
- Eligible entities: S corps, C corps, partnerships, LLCs, sole proprietors. **Nonprofits and government entities are ineligible.**
- If the provider is primarily child-care focused, **at least 30% of enrollees must be dependents of the contracting employer's employees**.
- **Effective for tax years beginning after Dec 31, 2025.**
- DCAP amounts are **not** eligible 45F expenditures.

Source: [BPC, 45F Employer-Provided Child Care Tax Credit: 2026 Guide](https://bipartisanpolicy.org/explainer/45f-employer-provided-child-care-tax-credit-2026-guide/); [Mercer](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/insights/us-health-news/big-beautiful-bill-permanently-enhances-dependent-care-benefits/)

**Commercial read:** The pooled/joint-ownership change is the structural unlock. A single 260-center operator can now credibly market a *shared* on-site or near-site center to a consortium of mid-size employers in one office park or hospital campus, with each employer claiming its allocable share of a 40–50% credit up to $500K–$600K. Before 2026 that structure did not qualify.

**Open items requiring Treasury/IRS guidance** (BPC identifies five, notably): **allocation methodology among joint venturers** and **substantiation requirements**. **No Treasury or IRS guidance implementing the 45F amendments was located — UNCONFIRMED whether any exists.** IRS pages were not retrievable in this session. Confirm with tax counsel before structuring pooled deals; the allocation rules are the gating question.

### 4.2 Child and Dependent Care Tax Credit (IRC §21)
- Maximum applicable percentage rises **35% → 50%**.
- Phase-down restructured: 50% up to $15,000 AGI, stepping down; a uniform ~15-percentage-point increase across most income ranges; floor remains **20%** above roughly $103,000 AGI ($206,000 joint).
- **Eligible expense caps unchanged: $3,000 (one dependent) / $6,000 (two or more).**
- **Not indexed for inflation.**
- **Effective for tax year 2026.** ([Mercer](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/insights/us-health-news/big-beautiful-bill-permanently-enhances-dependent-care-benefits/); [H&R Block](https://www.hrblock.com/tax-center/irs/tax-law-and-policy/one-big-beautiful-bill-families/))
- *Note a source conflict:* [WesternCPE](https://www.westerncpe.com/taxbyte/the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-delivers-first-dependent-care-updates-in-decades/) states the §21 change is effective TY2025 and gives a different phase-down floor (35% at $45,000–$206,000). Two other sources say TY2026 with a 20% floor. **I read TY2026 as correct but flag the discrepancy — verify with tax counsel before using in client-facing marketing.**

## 5. WORKFORCE, LABOR, AND IMMIGRATION POLICY AFFECTING STAFFING

### 5.1 DOL overtime rule
- The Biden-era rule, *"Defining and Delimiting the Exemptions for Executive, Administrative, Professional, Outside Sales, and Computer Employees,"* published **April 26, 2024** (doc 2024-08038), raised salary thresholds with automatic updating. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/04/26/2024-08038/defining-and-delimiting-the-exemptions-for-executive-administrative-professional-outside-sales-and))
- **DOL's current published guidance reflects the pre-2024 thresholds: $684/week ($35,568/year) for the EAP exemption and $107,432/year for highly compensated employees** — WHD Fact Sheet 17A, revision date September 2019. ([DOL Fact Sheet 17A](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/17a-overtime))
- **A Federal Register query of all EAP-exemption rulemakings returned no DOL rule or proposed rule on this topic after April 2024.** There is no new overtime rulemaking published as of Aug 3, 2026.
- The nationwide vacatur of the 2024 rule (reported as *Texas v. DOL*, E.D. Tex., Nov. 2024) is the generally understood reason the thresholds reverted, but **I could not confirm the case citation or DOL's litigation posture from a primary source in this session — treat the case citation as UNCONFIRMED.** The operative fact — $684/week applies and no new rule is pending in the Federal Register — is confirmed.
- **Planning implication:** assistant directors and center directors classified as exempt at salaries between $35,568 and $58,656 are not currently at federal risk. Monitor for a new DOL NPRM; none is published.

### 5.2 Federal minimum wage and contractor wage
- **Executive Order 14026** (federal contractor minimum wage, issued April 27, 2021) was **revoked March 14, 2025 by Executive Order 14236**, *"Additional Rescissions of Harmful Executive Orders and Actions."* DOL is **no longer enforcing EO 14026 or 29 CFR part 23** and is moving to rescind the regulations. ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/government-contracts/eo14026))
- **This matters directly if you hold or pursue federal contracts — including DoD child care fee-assistance arrangements.** The contractor wage floor is gone.
- **No federal statutory minimum wage increase was identified.** The federal minimum remains at the long-standing statutory level; **no 2025–2026 federal minimum wage action was located — UNCONFIRMED that none occurred.**

### 5.3 Immigration enforcement and the ECE workforce
- **Approximately 20% of the early care and education workforce is immigrant.** ([CLASP, April 23, 2026](https://www.clasp.org/press-room/press-releases/new-reports-lead-experts-elevate-harms-of-2025-immigration-operations-on-children-and-early-care-providers/))
- CLASP research (focus groups **June–December 2025**; 56 immigrant parents / 74 children under 6; interviews with 67 early educators across **Colorado, Georgia, Illinois, Michigan, New Jersey, Texas, Washington**) documents **significant enrollment declines**, including in Head Start, driven by family fear; historic increases in ICE funding enabling doubled detention and deportation activity; and measurable child behavioral/developmental effects. Provider quote: *"Immigration enforcement doesn't just stay out in the streets, it walks"* into classrooms.
- **PRWORA reclassification:** HHS notice *"Interpretation of 'Federal Public Benefit,'"* **90 FR 31232, July 14, 2025** (doc 2025-13118), **effective immediately** notwithstanding a comment period closing Aug 13, 2025. It added **Head Start** to the list of federal public benefits (12 new HHS programs total, also including Title X, CSBG, Health Center Program, several behavioral health programs, and Title IV-E programs). **The Child Care and Development Fund was already designated in the 1998 notice and remains designated.** ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/07/14/2025-13118/personal-responsibility-and-work-opportunity-reconciliation-act-of-1996-prwora-interpretation-of))
- Parallel notices: **Department of Education, July 11, 2025** (doc 2025-12925); **DOJ, July 16, 2025** (doc 2025-13318); **HUD, Nov 26, 2025** (doc 2025-21120).
- **Litigation:** CLASP reports the **ACLU of Washington obtained preliminary injunctive relief halting implementation** of the eligibility restrictions. ([CLASP, June 17, 2026](https://www.clasp.org/blog/understanding-immigrant-eligibility-for-child-care-and-early-education-programs/)) **Case name, court, docket number, and current scope of the injunction are UNCONFIRMED** — I could not retrieve the case record.
- **Related DHS actions in 2026:** *Public Charge Ground of Inadmissibility* **final rule published July 20, 2026** (doc 2026-14539) rescinding the 2022 public charge regulations; *Establishing a Fixed Time Period of Admission...for Nonimmigrant Academic Students, Exchange Visitors...* final rule **July 17, 2026** (doc 2026-14439). Both bear on immigrant families' willingness to enroll and on visa-based staffing pipelines.
- **Operational implication:** in centers with high immigrant-family census, budget for enrollment volatility that is not price- or capacity-driven. In centers with high immigrant staffing, I-9 and E-Verify hygiene and a documented enforcement-response protocol are now a material operating risk control, not a compliance formality.

### 5.4 Background checks
- Federal requirements sit at **45 C.F.R. § 98.43** (2016 CCDF rule) and CCDBG Act §658H. **Eight components: three in-state checks, two national checks, three interstate checks.**
- Applies to any staff member employed for compensation — **including contract employees and self-employed individuals** — whose activities involve care or supervision of children, or who has **unsupervised access** to children. States may exempt relatives.
- Disqualifying: refusal to participate, submitting false information, sex offender registry listing, conviction for specified crimes.
- States must publicly post their background check policies and procedures.
- ([Child Care Technical Assistance Network / ACF](https://childcareta.acf.hhs.gov/ccdf-fundamentals/comprehensive-background-checks))
- **No federal changes to background-check requirements in 2025–2026 were identified.** The 2026 CCDF final rule did not address them. Recheck frequency and provider appeal rights were not detailed in the retrievable source — **UNCONFIRMED**.

## 6. OTHER FEDERAL LEVERS

### 6.1 Preschool Development Grants B-5
- **FY2026: $315M**, level funded and preserved against the Administration's proposed elimination.
- **FY2027: proposed for elimination by both the President's Budget and the House Labor-HHS bill.** The Senate has historically protected it.

### 6.3 DoD / military child care fee assistance
- **MilitaryChildCare.com** is the central portal. **Military Child Care in Your Neighborhood (MCCYN)** is the fee-assistance program for families who cannot access installation care; it is **administered by Child Care Aware® of America**. An expanded **MCCYN-PLUS** operates through partnerships with the **Department of War** (the renamed DoD). ([Military OneSource](https://www.militaryonesource.mil/family/child-care/))
- Referral logic: installation-based care is prioritized when families live within ~30 minutes of a base; otherwise families are routed to Child Care Aware and a national caregiver database.
- **Provider quality/accreditation requirements, MCCYN-PLUS's use of state QRIS ratings in lieu of national accreditation, branch-by-branch differences, and any 2025–2026 expansion details are UNCONFIRMED** — Child Care Aware's fee-assistance pages did not return substantive content.

### 6.4 ARPA stabilization — the cliff and its aftermath
- ARPA child care funds are **fully expired**. The Century Foundation's pre-cliff projection was that **3.2 million children would likely lose spots** when federal funds ended. ([TCF, June 21, 2023](https://tcf.org/content/report/child-care-cliff/))
- Post-cliff documentation: [*"With Arrival of Child Care Cliff, Some States Have Stepped In to Save the Sector"*](https://tcf.org/content/report/with-arrival-of-child-care-cliff-some-states-have-stepped-in-to-save-the-sector/) (Jan 17, 2024) and [*"Child Care Funding Cliff at One Year: Rising Prices, Shrinking Options, and Families Squeezed"*](https://tcf.org/content/report/child-care-funding-cliff-at-one-year/) (Sept 24, 2024).
- **The specific ARPA dollar amounts ($24B stabilization / $15B CCDF supplemental) and the exact obligation/liquidation deadlines could not be re-confirmed from a primary source in this session — UNCONFIRMED as stated figures.** What is confirmed is that the cliff arrived, that some states backfilled with state dollars, and that the sector has operated without federal stabilization support since.
- **Current relevance:** three years post-cliff, the state backfills are what is now under pressure. Missouri's pause on prospective payments and Washington's elimination of enrollment-based payment funding are the leading indicators of state fiscal retrenchment reaching your reimbursement terms.
