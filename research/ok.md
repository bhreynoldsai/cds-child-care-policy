# OKLAHOMA — Child Care Policy, Regulation & Funding Profile
**Prepared:** August 3, 2026 · **For:** multi-state for-profit child care operator · **Sources:** primary state/federal documents where available; secondary sources flagged

---

## EXECUTIVE ORIENTATION

Oklahoma is, as of mid-2026, one of the more **volatile** state environments in the country for a for-profit operator, and the volatility cuts both ways:

- **Negative:** The subsidy program is in active retrenchment. The pandemic-era $5/day add-on ended April 6, 2026; income eligibility drops from 85% to **55% of State Median Income effective July 1, 2026** — about a month before this report. Base reimbursement rates have not been revised since **January 1, 2023**. [OK Policy reports **131 child care centers have closed since April 2026**](https://okpolicy.org/oklahoma-children-deserve-investment-when-will-lawmakers-act/).
- **Positive:** Two structural wins for operators landed July 1, 2026 via **HB 4298** — authority to charge subsidy families a **differential/gap fee**, and a mandate that OKDHS **loosen master teacher ratios**. Separately, the federal **"Restoring Flexibility" CCDF rule (effective July 13, 2026)** rescinded the 7% copay cap, enrollment-based payment, and prospective payment mandates — removing the federal ceiling that would have blocked Oklahoma's gap-fee model.
- **Distinctive:** Oklahoma's near-universal school-based pre-K is **shrinking** — 65% of 4-year-olds in 2024-25, down from 76% pre-pandemic — and NIEER's own analysis attributes part of the decline to *children staying in child care centers*. That is a demand tailwind for private operators, and it is the single most operator-relevant trend in the state.

## 1. LICENSING & REGULATION

### Agency and authority
Licensing sits with **Oklahoma Human Services (OKDHS), Child Care Services** under [OAC Title 340:110-3](https://www.publichealthlawcenter.org/sites/default/files/Oklahoma%20Regulations_Requirements%20For%20Child%20Care%20Centers%20(340_110-3-1%20%E2%80%93%20340_110-3-33.3).pdf) and 10 O.S. §404. Contact: (405) 521-3561 / 1-844-834-8314. [Program page](https://oklahoma.gov/okdhs/services/child-care-services.html).

**Note for counsel:** OKDHS's own policy-library URLs (`oklahoma.gov/okdhs/library/policy/current/oac-340/...`) now **302-redirect to [rules.ok.gov](https://rules.ok.gov/home)**. Oklahoma has migrated its administrative code to a new platform. Any internal compliance links your team maintains to the old OKDHS policy library are broken.

### Governing document
**Pub 14-05, "Licensing Requirements for Child Care Programs," effective November 1, 2025** (posted 07/31/2025) — [PDF](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/okdhs/documents/okdhs-publication-library/14-05_LicensingRequirementsforChildCarePrograms_ccs_07312025.pdf). Covers centers, day camps, drop-in, out-of-school-time, part-day, and sick-child programs. Homes are governed separately by [Pub 86-104](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/okdhs/documents/okdhs-publication-library/86-104.pdf) (family homes up to 7; large homes 8–12). The [licensing requirements index page](https://oklahoma.gov/okdhs/services/child-care-services/child-care-licensing/licensing-requirements.html) shows a last-modified date of **February 25, 2026**. The Spanish version of 14-05 is still the **June 2022** edition — a compliance gap if you serve Spanish-dominant staff.

### Staff qualifications and training
Per [Pub 14-05 (eff. 11/1/2025)](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/okdhs/documents/okdhs-publication-library/14-05_LicensingRequirementsforChildCarePrograms_ccs_07312025.pdf):

**Directors** — 21+; current **bronze or higher Oklahoma Director's Credential**; must work at least 50% of operating hours or 30 hours/week, whichever is less; CPR/first aid within 3 months.

**Master Teachers** — 18+; HS diploma/GED; **Level 4+ Oklahoma Professional Development Ladder** certificate; CPR/first aid current; **minimum one master teacher per 60 children of licensed capacity**.

> ⚠️ **This is the provision HB 4298 targets.** Under legacy Reaching for the Stars criteria the master-teacher ratio tightened to **1:30 for Two- and Three-Star centers** ([Pub 99-39](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/okdhs/documents/okdhs-publication-library/99-39.pdf)). HB 4298 (effective 7/1/2026) directs OKDHS to establish **more flexible master teacher ratios**. Whether OKDHS has yet promulgated the implementing rule is **UNCONFIRMED** — this is a live action item (see What to Watch).

**All teaching personnel** — **Entry Level Child Care Training (ELCCT) within 90 calendar days of hire**; CPR/first aid within 3 months; current Professional Development Ladder certification; **annual infection control training**.

### Background checks
Comprehensive background investigation required, including criminal history (**resubmitted every 5 years**) and a search of the **Restricted Registry ("Joshua's List")**. Disqualifiers include felony convictions for crimes against children, sex offenses, **drug-related offenses within 5 years**, and other violent crimes.

### Inspection and enforcement
- Fire inspections **at least every two years** by local/state fire authority; health inspections likewise by the Oklahoma State Department of Health or Indian Health Services.
- OKDHS licensing visits are logged publicly on the **[Child Care Locator](https://childcarefind.okdhs.org/)** (also at [ccl.dhs.ok.gov](https://ccl.dhs.ok.gov/)) with **Visit Type** (Full / Partial / Attempted), **Purpose** (Periodic / Follow Up / Complaint / Other), a count of areas in compliance, itemized non-compliances, and full downloadable reports — plus a **substantiated complaint history**. [Example provider record](https://ccl.dhs.ok.gov/providers/K830056455). **Reputational note:** substantiated complaints are public, itemized, and persistent (the example shows history back to September 2023). Multi-site operators should assume competitors and journalists can pull center-level compliance records trivially.
- Per the [rules & feedback page](https://oklahoma.gov/okdhs/services/adult/ccsubsidy/rulesandfeedback.html), **accredited programs are exempt from partial reviews** absent serious non-compliance — a concrete operational benefit to accreditation beyond star level.

## 2. SUBSIDY / CCDF

### Rate methodology and current rates
Oklahoma uses **tiered reimbursement keyed directly to Reaching for the Stars star level** — the tie is unusually steep, which makes star level the single largest driver of subsidy revenue per child.

**Appendix C-4-B, Child Care Provider Rate Schedule — effective January 1, 2023** ([PDF](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/okdhs/documents/searchcenter/okdhsformresults/c-4-b.pdf)). **I found no evidence of a rate revision since January 1, 2023.** Base rates have therefore been frozen for roughly 3.5 years through a period in which [infant care costs rose 27% between 2023 and 2025](https://www.kccu.org/show/stateimpact-oklahoma/2026-06-12/the-cost-of-childcare-is-too-high-for-many-oklahoma-families-how-are-lawmakers-responding).

**Center full-time daily rates:**

| Age band | 1★ | 2★ | 3★ | 4★ | 5★ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–12 mo | $20.00 | $22.80 | $38.00 | $44.00 | **$50.60** |
| 13–24 mo | $20.00 | $21.80 | $33.60 | $41.80 | $48.10 |
| 25–48 mo | $17.20 | $19.60 | $31.50 | $35.90 | $41.30 |
| 49–72 mo | $17.00 | $18.50 | $22.80 | $25.20 | $29.00 |
| 73 mo–13 yr | $13.80 | $16.10 | $18.80 | $20.70 | $23.80 |

**The 2★→3★ jump is the money cliff:** infant care goes from $22.80 to $38.00/day — a **67% increase** for one star level. Moving from 1★ to 5★ on infants is **+153%**. Any Oklahoma portfolio strategy should be built around star advancement first and volume second.

**Add-ons and differentials:**
- Special needs (ages 0–19): **moderate +$16 FT / +$12 PT per day**; **severe +$28 FT / +$20 PT per day**
- **Non-traditional hours: $14/day** (full-time only)
- **Child welfare case: +$5/day**
- Community Hope Centers: $25 FT / $13 PT / $15 blended
- Part-time and "traditional blended" / "extended blended" rates exist for school-age and 49–72 mo bands

### The 2026 retrenchment sequence
[OKDHS announcement, January 7, 2026](https://oklahoma.gov/okdhs/newsroom/2026/january/comm01072026.html):

| Effective | Change |
|---|---|
| **Jan 12, 2026** | Subsidy access **expanded** to children ages **6, 7, and 8**; TANF emergency-assistance families qualify **up to age 13** |
| **Apr 6, 2026** | **$5/day pandemic add-on ends for all recipients** — federal pandemic funds exhausted |
| **Jul 1, 2026** | Income eligibility **reverts from 85% to 55% of State Median Income** |

The 55% SMI reversion is estimated to affect **approximately 5,800 families** ([KGOU, Jan 29, 2026](https://www.kgou.org/health/2026-01-29/oklahoma-human-services-asks-state-legislature-for-nearly-70-million-to-fund-child-care)). During the 2025 federal shutdown OKDHS had also **halted new applications and renewals**, partially restored in January 2026.

**Fiscal backdrop:** the subsidy program historically costs **~$280M/year against $230M budgeted**, with the gap covered by now-exhausted federal TANF reserves. Director Cartmell said the ask would let OKDHS "continue to serve the **40,000 kids** on the subsidy program that we were serving at the end of September."

### Eligibility and copay
**Appendix C-4, Eligibility/Copayment Chart — effective October 1, 2025** ([PDF](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/okdhs/documents/searchcenter/okdhsformresults/c-4.pdf)). Federal ceiling is 85% SMI. Monthly income maximums as of that chart:

| Family size | Max monthly income |
|---|---|
| 2 | $4,525 |
| 3 | $5,589 |
| 4 | $6,654 |
| 5 | $7,718 |
| 6 | $8,783 |

Copays range **$0 to $473/month**. **The copay is set at initial approval and is not increased until the next eligibility renewal** — a stability feature for providers. **Note:** this chart predates the July 1, 2026 shift to 55% SMI; a revised C-4 should exist but I could not confirm one. **UNCONFIRMED — verify current C-4.**

### Payment mechanics — critical operational detail
> **"OKDHS does NOT begin paying until services provided total more than the client co-payment."** — [2026 Child Care Provider Pay Schedule](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/okdhs/documents/searchcenter/okdhsformresults/10ad007e.pdf)

Oklahoma runs a **copay-first** model: the provider must collect the family copay before any state dollars flow. For a family at the $473/month copay ceiling this is a substantial collection risk borne entirely by the operator. Combined with the July 2026 eligibility cut, expect elevated bad debt. The pay schedule also flags holiday payment delays and separate handling for absent pay / non-traditional rate payments.

**System change:** OKDHS is rolling out **KinderSystems** as a new provider payment system with attendance tracking and real-time payment visibility ("Coming soon" per the [providers & educators page](https://oklahoma.gov/okdhs/services/child-care-services/providers-educators.html)). Timing **UNCONFIRMED** — worth pre-positioning your billing operations.

### Federal CCDF rule — the landscape flipped in 2026
The 2024 CCDF final rule that drove states toward enrollment-based payment and a 7% copay cap has been **substantially rescinded**.

**"Restoring Flexibility in the Child Care and Development Fund (CCDF)," final rule published May 12, 2026, [effective July 13, 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/12/2026-09382/restoring-flexibility-in-the-child-care-and-development-fund-ccdf).** It rescinds four 2024 requirements:

| Provision | 2024 rule | After July 13, 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| §98.45(l)(3) | Copays capped at **7% of family income** | **Rescinded** — states need only use a sliding scale and avoid barriers |
| §98.45(m)(2) | Pay based on **authorized enrollment** | **Rescinded** — attendance-based payment permitted; delinking from occasional absences "to the extent practicable" |
| §98.45(m)(1) | **Prospective** payment required | **Rescinded** — prospective **or** within **21 calendar days** of invoice |
| §98.30(b)(1) | Some direct services via **grants/contracts** | **Rescinded** — optional |

See also [NAEYC's summary](https://www.naeyc.org/our-work/public-policy-advocacy/2026-ccdf-final-rule).

**Strategic read:** the removal of the 7% federal copay cap is what makes Oklahoma's HB 4298 gap-fee model (10% of household income) legally viable. Simultaneously, the rescission of enrollment-based payment removes federal pressure on Oklahoma to move off attendance-based reimbursement — a revenue-stability negative for operators. Oklahoma's FY2025–2027 CCDF Plan is on file with ACF ([approved plans](https://acf.gov/occ/form/approved-ccdf-plans-fy-2025-2027)); **whether Oklahoma has filed an amendment responding to the 2026 rule is UNCONFIRMED.**

### Waitlist
**UNCONFIRMED.** No source reviewed documented a formal Oklahoma subsidy waitlist. Oklahoma instead rationed via the 2025 application/renewal freeze and the 2026 eligibility reduction. Confirm directly with OKDHS.

### Child care workforce eligibility
Oklahoma provides **no-cost child care to early childhood educators** under **HB 2778 (2025)** — see §5. The related **Oklahoma Strong Start Program** has awarded **$2.37 million in benefits to nearly 1,000 child care professionals** ([StateImpact, June 12, 2026](https://www.kccu.org/show/stateimpact-oklahoma/2026-06-12/the-cost-of-childcare-is-too-high-for-many-oklahoma-families-how-are-lawmakers-responding)). OKDHS requested **$11.5M** for Strong Start in FY27 and received roughly **$4.5M**. A dedicated OKDHS page for worker-specific subsidy eligibility returned 404; **program mechanics are UNCONFIRMED** and should be sourced directly. This is a **material recruiting tool** — free child care for your own staff is worth real money against $11–12/hour wages.

## 3. STATE PRE-K — the defining Oklahoma dynamic

### Structure
Oklahoma runs one of the nation's oldest near-universal 4-year-old pre-K programs, **delivered through public school districts**. Children who turn **4 by September 1** are eligible; **no income requirement**. Programs use an **Early Childhood Certified Teacher**, a **10:1 child-to-teacher ratio**, state-adopted curriculum standards, and offer **both full-day and half-day** options. Governing rule: **OAC 210:15-11-2**. ([OSDE](https://oklahoma.gov/education/services/standards-learning/early-childhood-education/four-year-old-pre-k.html))

Per NIEER, **100% of non-charter districts** offer the state program. ([NIEER Oklahoma profile](https://nieer.org/yearbook/2024/state-profiles/oklahoma))

### Enrollment is falling — and that matters commercially

| Metric | 2019-20 | 2023-24 | 2024-25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4-year-olds enrolled | 39,767 | 38,457 | **32,794** |
| % of 4-year-olds served | 76% (peak, thru 2018-19) | — | **65%** |
| 3-year-olds enrolled | 2,111 | — | **1,370** (3%) |

Source: NIEER *State of Preschool* yearbooks via [KGOU, April 23, 2026](https://www.kgou.org/education/2026-04-23/national-preschool-enrollment-surged-last-year-oklahomas-continued-to-fall) and the [NIEER Oklahoma profile](https://nieer.org/yearbook/2024/state-profiles/oklahoma). ([2025 NIEER Yearbook PDF](https://nieer.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/2025%20NIEER%20Yearbook.pdf))

**That is a ~7,000-child, 17.6% decline in five years — while national preschool enrollment surged.**

**Rankings (2024-25):** 5th nationally for 4-year-old access (behind DC, Vermont, Colorado, West Virginia); 27th for 3-year-olds; **30th in state spending per child ($5,749)**; 10th in all-funding-sources spending ($12,433); meets **9 of 10** NIEER quality benchmarks (missing only the assistant teacher credential benchmark). 2023-24 state spending was **$197,385,755** at **$5,133/child**.

**NIEER's stated causes of the decline include, explicitly, "children remaining in childcare centers through ages 4-5,"** alongside low awareness (pre-K is free but not required), district-level enrollment friction, safety/readiness concerns, declining overall K-12 enrollment, increased homeschooling, and "declining trust" in public education.

**Commercial read:** the structural threat that school-based UPK poses to private 4-year-old enrollment is **weakening in Oklahoma**, not strengthening. Roughly 3,500 4-year-olds per year have shifted out of the district pre-K pipeline since 2019. For a multi-site operator this argues for **holding or expanding 4-year-old capacity** rather than ceding the age band — the opposite of the correct posture in most UPK states.

### The partnership pathway — real, but thinly documented
State law **explicitly permits public school early childhood programs to operate collaboratively with other organizations.** OSDE names documented partner types: **Child Care Centers**, Head Start, Educare, corporate facilities, and assisted living centers, under **OAC 210:15-11-2**. ([OSDE](https://oklahoma.gov/education/services/standards-learning/early-childhood-education/four-year-old-pre-k.html))

The best-documented collaboration model is the **Tulsa Head Start / Tulsa Public Schools partnership** ([HeadStart.gov case study](https://headstart.gov/local-early-childhood-partnerships/article/head-start-pre-k-local-partnerships-work-tulsa-oklahoma)). Oklahoma's collaboration model was also profiled historically in [Pre-K Now's "Beyond the School Yard"](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED542900) and the [Harvard Zaentz Early Education Initiative's Oklahoma profile](https://zaentznavigator.gse.harvard.edu/policy-strategies/four-year-old-pre-k/).

**Mechanics for a private operator (partially UNCONFIRMED):** In the collaboration model the **school district remains the fiscal and accountability agent**, drawing state aid for the child, and contracts with the community site. The binding constraint is the **Early Childhood Certified Teacher requirement** — the teacher of record in a collaborative classroom must hold Oklahoma ECE certification (bachelor's + specialized early childhood training), and is typically **employed by the district and placed in your classroom**, or employed by you at district-comparable salary. NIEER characterizes Oklahoma's delivery as public-school-based rather than an explicitly mixed-delivery model, which suggests collaboration is **permissive and district-discretionary rather than a funded, standardized statewide contracting channel**.

**I could not locate a current OSDE policy document, application, per-pupil pass-through rate, or list of active collaborative sites.** **This is the largest single research gap in this profile.** Given declining district pre-K enrollment, districts have *reduced* incentive to expand collaborations — but *increased* incentive to preserve headcount, which cuts the other way. **Recommended action:** direct outreach to OSDE Early Childhood Education and to the specific districts where you operate (Tulsa, OKC, Moore, Deer Creek, Edmond, Norman) to establish whether a contracting pathway is live and what the per-child economics are. The certified-teacher salary requirement is likely the deal-breaker or deal-maker.

## 4. QUALITY RATING SYSTEM — Reaching for the Stars

### Current structure (restructured effective June 1, 2022)
Oklahoma moved to an **upgraded five-level system with more incentives** effective **June 1, 2022**, adding "a new path to accreditation for providers seeking to attain a **4 or 5-star rating**," criteria **based on staff retention**, and a simplified online application. ([OKDHS QRIS page](https://oklahoma.gov/okdhs/services/child-care-services/qris.html))

Evaluation domains: curriculum, child outcomes, learning environments, teacher-child interactions, professional development, and training.

> ⚠️ **Stale-source warning.** The [ACF/OPRE Oklahoma QRS profile](https://acf.gov/opre/report/oklahoma-reaching-stars-qrs-profile), the [ECQuality state page](https://ecquality.acf.hhs.gov/states/oklahoma), and [OKDHS Pub 99-39](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/okdhs/documents/okdhs-publication-library/99-39.pdf) (dated **November 1, 2016**) all still describe the **legacy four-level system** — One Star, One Star Plus, Two Star, Three Star. That structure is **obsolete**. Live provider records confirm the current nomenclature: a sampled center displays "**4 Star Level Program**" ([ccl.dhs.ok.gov](https://ccl.dhs.ok.gov/providers/K830056455)), and the current rate schedule pays across One through Five Star. Do not let vendors or consultants brief you off the ACF profile.

### Legacy criteria still informative
From Pub 99-39: the foundation of RFTS is licensing compliance — programs must show no "numerous, repeated, or serious" non-compliance over **24 months**. Higher levels require master teacher ratios (**1:30 at Two/Three Star** under the legacy scheme), director and staff professional development documentation, structured learning environments with defined interest areas, family engagement activities and conferences, and formal program evaluation using approved assessment tools. **Enhanced-area counties pay higher rates at every star level.** ([Interest areas guidance](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/okdhs/documents/okdhs-pdf-library/child-care-services/reachingforthestarsandinterestcentersteacherinformation_dcc_05092007.pdf))

**Current 4/5-star criteria documents could not be retrieved. UNCONFIRMED.**

### The accreditation mandate — the biggest QRIS change
Per the December 2024 emergency rules ([KOSU](https://www.kosu.org/local-news/2025-01-10/oklahoma-dhs-weighs-changes-to-accreditation-requirements-for-child-care-providers)):
- **Five-star facilities must obtain accreditation from one of nine approved national organizations**
- Facilities approved before the effective date: proof of accreditation or application within **60 calendar days** (milestone **April 10, 2025**)
- Accreditation process completion by **September 1, 2025**; final deadline **December 31, 2025**
- **Failure = star rating reduction** (and, given the rate table, a **~13% revenue cut** on infants dropping 5★→4★, or **~48%** dropping 5★→3★)
- **All costs borne by providers**; providers testified the process takes **~2 years**

Also adopted: **quality coaches** now handle QRIS monitoring; **accredited programs are exempt from partial reviews** absent serious non-compliance; annual criteria reviews available on one week's notice.

**Related:** RFTS requirements were updated to **reduce non-educational screen time** in family child care homes ([CHOICES Project brief](https://choicesproject.org/publications/brief-ece-screen-time-oklahoma/)) — homes only, but signals direction of travel for center standards.

## 5. LEGISLATION — 2025 and 2026 sessions

### 2025 session

**HB 2778 — ENACTED OVER VETO.** Authors **Rep. Suzanne Schreiber (D-Tulsa)** and **Sen. Chuck Hall (R-Perry)**. Provides **no-cost child care to early childhood educators** as a recruitment/retention incentive. Governor **Stitt vetoed**; the Legislature **overrode on June 3, 2025** — **House 69-17, Senate 36-9**. The policy was originally drafted as **HB 1849** and moved into budget vehicle HB 2778. Rationale: early childhood educators earn **$11–12/hour** and often cannot afford their own child care. ([Oklahoma Business Voice](https://okbusinessvoice.com/2025/06/03/childcare-bill-now-oklahoma-law-after-legislature-overrides-stitt-veto/))

*The override is the single most important political datapoint in this profile: a Republican supermajority overrode a Republican governor on a child care bill by better than 4:1 in the House. Child care has escaped partisan valence in Oklahoma.*

**HB 1848 — FAILED.** Would have created a **30% employer tax credit** for businesses offering child care subsidies or operating child care facilities, capped at **$30,000 per employer** and **$5 million annually**, for tax years **2026–2030**. Did not pass. ([Oklahoma Business Voice](https://okbusinessvoice.com/2025/06/03/childcare-bill-now-oklahoma-law-after-legislature-overrides-stitt-veto/))

**Committee activity:** the Senate **Business & Insurance Committee** pursued child care reform paired with unemployment tax reduction in January 2025 — notable because it framed child care as a *business* rather than *human services* issue. ([Oklahoma Business Voice, Jan 10, 2025](https://okbusinessvoice.com/?s=child+care))

### 2026 session (convened February 2026; adjourned late May)

**HB 4298 — SIGNED MAY 28, 2026; EFFECTIVE JULY 1, 2026.** Authors **Rep. Suzanne Schreiber (D-Tulsa)** and **Sen. Paul Rosino (R-OKC)**. **The most operator-relevant bill in the state.** ([OKW News](https://okwnews.com/news/whatzup/state/schreiber-rosino-bill-to-support-childcare-industry-becomes-law/))

1. **Master teacher ratio flexibility** — OKDHS must establish more flexible master teacher ratios while maintaining overall staff-to-child ratios. Pure margin relief: master teachers require Level 4+ PD Ladder credentials and command premium wages.
2. **Differential / gap fee authority** — subject to federal approval, licensed providers may charge subsidy families the **gap between the state subsidy rate and private-pay rate**, provided **total charges to the parent (copay + gap fee) do not exceed 10% of household income**.

Sen. Rosino: the bill "strikes a balance by granting financial relief to providers without eliminating guardrails that help keep costs in check for families." Rep. Schreiber said ratio requirements have strained centers "already struggling to keep their doors open." **40 other states already permit similar differential fees**, and federal approval is expected.

**Compounding pressures:**
- Oklahoma appropriated **$85M for a $2,000 raise to public school teacher minimum salaries** in FY27, widening the K-12 vs. child care wage gap and intensifying poaching of your credentialed staff.
