# ALABAMA — Child Care Policy, Regulation & Funding Profile
**Prepared 3 August 2026 | For a multi-state for-profit child care operator**

---

## 0. EXECUTIVE ORIENTATION

Alabama is a **low-regulation, low-subsidy, high-pre-K-quality** state with one unusually operator-friendly feature: it is one of the few states whose child care tax credit is written **explicitly for for-profit providers**. The binding constraints for a for-profit multi-site operator are (a) subsidy reimbursement well below market, (b) a stagnant licensing rulebook that has not been comprehensively revised since 2021, and (c) a **December 31, 2027 sunset** on the tax credit package that makes the March 2027 session the single most important date on the calendar.

**Correction to a common planning assumption:** the Alabama Legislature does **not** convene in January 2027. Under the four-year-term rotation, the first year of a term convenes the **first Tuesday in March** — so the 2027 Regular Session begins **March 2, 2027**. ([Ballotpedia — Alabama State Legislature](https://ballotpedia.org/Alabama_State_Legislature)) This is confirmed by the pattern: 2026 (4th year) convened January 13, 2026; 2025 (3rd year) February 4, 2025.

## 1. LICENSING & REGULATION

### Agency and rule base
Child care licensing sits with the **Alabama Department of Human Resources (DHR), Child Care Services Division**. ([DHR Licensing Overview](https://dhr.alabama.gov/child-care/licensing-overview/))

The governing rule is **Ala. Admin. Code ch. 660-5-26, "Day Care Licensure – Child Care Licensing and Performance Standards for Day Care Centers and Nighttime Centers."** The current version took **effect September 13, 2021** (published July 30, 2021). It covers definitions, licensing procedure, facility requirements, program standards, transportation, staff qualifications, character/suitability, administration, nighttime centers, corrective action, and hearings. ([Alabama Administrative Code 660-5-26](https://admincode.legislature.state.al.us/api/chapter/660-5-26))

**Finding: no comprehensive revision to the day care center minimum standards was adopted in 2025 or 2026.** The 2021 rewrite remains the operative standard. I found no pending proposed rulemaking for centers in 2025–2026. *(Confidence: moderate — DHR does not publish a consolidated rulemaking docket; a targeted APA notice search would firm this up.)*

Licenses **must be renewed every two years**. ([DHR Licensing Overview](https://dhr.alabama.gov/child-care/licensing-overview/))

### Staff-to-child ratios (centers)
Effective July 8, 2005 and unchanged since ([DHR Day Care Center License Requirements](https://dhr.alabama.gov/child-care/day-care-center-license-requirements/)):

| Age group | Ratio |
|---|---|
| 0–18 months | 1:5 |
| 18 months – 2½ years | 1:7 |
| 24–36 months | 1:8 |
| 2½ – 4 years | 1:11 |
| 4 years – school age | 1:18 |
| School age – 8 years | 1:21 |
| 8 years and older | 1:22 |

Ratios must be maintained **at all times**, with distinct ratios for swimming/wading, napping, transportation, and nighttime care.

**Group size:** Alabama does **not publish separate maximum group-size caps** alongside its ratio table in the DHR consumer materials. *(Marked UNCONFIRMED — group size limits, if any, would sit inside the 660-5-26 facility/program rules text, which I could not render in full.)* Note this is a **competitive advantage** relative to states with hard group-size caps: Alabama's 1:18 for 4-year-olds and 1:11 for 2½–4s are among the more permissive in the Southeast.

### Staff qualifications and training
([Alabama Childcare Facts — Minimum Standards](https://alabamachildcarefacts.com/minimum-standards/))

- **Teachers:** high school diploma or GED, plus **≥12 clock hours** of child care/development training annually.
- **Directors** — one of four pathways:
  1. HS diploma/GED + 120 clock hours training + 12 months experience
  2. CDA or CCCA credential + HS diploma/GED + 12 months experience
  3. Associate degree in CD/ECE + 9 months experience
  4. Bachelor's degree in CD/ECE + 6 months experience
- Directors additionally need **≥20 clock hours** of administration/management training and **≥4 hours** of quality child care training annually.
- At least one staff member must hold current **infant-child First Aid and CPR** certification.

### Background checks
All staff undergo criminal background checks plus clearance from the **Alabama State Central Registry on Child Abuse/Neglect**. DHR's Child Care Services Division determines employment eligibility. ([DHR Licensing Overview](https://dhr.alabama.gov/child-care/licensing-overview/))

**Compliance flag:** ACF identified Alabama as **non-compliant with 45 CFR 98.44 (pediatric first aid pre-service training)** in the FY2025–2027 CCDF plan review. ([2025–2027 CCDF State Plan with Approval Letter](https://dhr.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/2025-2027-CCDF-State-Plan-with-Approval-Letter.pdf)) Expect corrective action to land as a **pre-service training requirement on new hires** — a real onboarding cost for a multi-site operator.

### Inspection and enforcement
DHR "investigates complaints" and is responsible for "recommending adverse action for day care centers and homes when non-compliance of standards warrant." ([DHR Licensing Overview](https://dhr.alabama.gov/child-care/licensing-overview/)) **Inspection frequency and enforcement-volume trend data are not published in accessible form — UNCONFIRMED.** A records request to DHR Child Care Services would be needed for adverse-action counts.

### Religious exemption — the 2018 Child Care Safety Act
**Still fully in force. No repeal or narrowing enacted in 2025 or 2026.**

Exemption is limited to preschool programs that are "an **integral part of a local church ministry or a religious nonprofit elementary school**." ([DHR License Exempt Day Care Facilities](https://dhr.alabama.gov/child-care/license-exempt-day-care-facilities/))

Exempt facilities must nonetheless:
- Notify DHR **≥30 days** before operating
- Provide annual notice to fire and health departments
- Submit employee names and criminal history information
- Provide proof of **property, casualty, and liability coverage**
- Maintain immunization verifications and medical histories
- Submit fire/health inspection reports within 30 days of receipt
- Disclose to parents: staff qualifications, staff ratios, discipline policy, curriculum type, religious teachings, meal programs
- **Post in plain view** a statement that the program is not regulated or licensed by DHR
- Obtain **annual signed parental affidavits** acknowledging exempt status
- File an annual facility compliance certification
- Permit DHR inspection whenever "reasonable cause" exists

**Operator implication:** exempt church-based centers remain a **price-competitive, lower-cost-structure competitor** in most Alabama markets. They cannot, however, access the Quality STARS-gated tax credits or (in practice) subsidy tiering, which is the operator's structural counter-advantage. Advocacy groups continue to press the point — VOICES notes "Alabama's child care system is not fully regulated. Exemptions for faith-based child care centers who receive no state or federal dollars are still allowed." ([KIDS COUNT — Child care facilities, Alabama](https://datacenter.aecf.org/data/tables/4652-child-care-facilities))

### Market size
DHR's 2023 market rate survey was mailed to **all 2,201 licensed and exempt child care providers** statewide — the best available proxy for total regulated-plus-exempt supply. ([2024 Child Care Cost Study / Market Rate Survey](https://dhr.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/2024-Child-Care-Cost-Study-Final-2024.05.17.pdf)) Disaggregated licensed-vs-exempt counts sit behind the KIDS COUNT download (data as of May 2025, last updated March 2026) — **UNCONFIRMED at the line-item level.**

## 2. SUBSIDY / CCDF — DHR Child Care Subsidy Program

### Plan status
Alabama's **FFY 2025–2027 CCDF State Plan was approved March 26, 2025.** ([2025–2027 CCDF State Plan with Approval Letter](https://dhr.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/2025-2027-CCDF-State-Plan-with-Approval-Letter.pdf))

Delivery runs through **four Child Care Management Agencies (CMAs) covering nine regions**. ([DHR Subsidy Overview](https://dhr.alabama.gov/child-care/subsidy-overview/))

### Eligibility
- **60% of State Median Income** for a family of three = **$3,729/month** gross
- Expressed alternatively as **180% of the Federal Poverty Level**
- Initial eligibility runs roughly **59–62% SMI** depending on family size, with a graduated phase-out tier at ~59% SMI; income limits effective January 1, 2024
- Parents must be employed and/or enrolled in school/training
([2025–2027 CCDF State Plan](https://dhr.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/2025-2027-CCDF-State-Plan-with-Approval-Letter.pdf); [FFY 2025–2027 CCDF Plan draft](https://dhr.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/CCDF-FFY-2025-2027-For-Alabama-2.pdf))

Note this is **well below the federal 85% SMI ceiling** — Alabama leaves substantial eligible demand unserved by design.

### Family copay and the federal 7% cap — an open compliance issue
- Sliding fee scale, actual copays range **2–5% of gross family income**
- **~60% of participating families pay no copayment**
- **Copays are assessed per child, not per family**, with no multi-child discount — meaning a family with two or three children in care **can exceed the 7% cap** in the 2024 federal CCDF final rule
- **Alabama has formally submitted a waiver** to come into compliance "after the start of the state plan cycle"
([2025–2027 CCDF State Plan](https://dhr.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/2025-2027-CCDF-State-Plan-with-Approval-Letter.pdf); [draft plan](https://dhr.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/CCDF-FFY-2025-2027-For-Alabama-2.pdf))

**Operator implication:** when Alabama does convert to a family-level 7% cap, per-child copay revenue at the center gate falls for multi-child families. Model this as a **receivables shift, not a revenue loss** — the state share should rise correspondingly, but timing risk is real.

### Payment basis — favorable
Alabama pays on an **enrollment basis, not attendance**. ([FFY 2025–2027 CCDF Plan](https://dhr.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/CCDF-FFY-2025-2027-For-Alabama-2.pdf)) This aligns with the 2024 federal rule and is materially better for occupancy-based unit economics than attendance-based states.

### Reimbursement rates and the market gap
The publicly posted rate schedule on DHR's site is **"Provider Rates with QRIS Tiers," dated April 1, 2022**. ([DHR Subsidy Overview](https://dhr.alabama.gov/child-care/subsidy-overview/)) Base rates were originally set at the **70th percentile** of the 2017 market rate survey, effective **October 1, 2018**. ([DHR — Child care subsidies rates to increase](https://dhr.alabama.gov/child-care-subsidies-rates-paid-by-the-department-of-human-resources-to-increase/))

**QRIS tiered add-ons above base rate:** 1 Star +2%, 2 Stars +4%, 3 Stars +6%, 4 Stars +8%, 5 Stars +10%. ([DHR rate release](https://dhr.alabama.gov/child-care-subsidies-rates-paid-by-the-department-of-human-resources-to-increase/)) *(Sourced to the 2018 release; still the structure referenced by DHR's rate document — treat as current but verify against the live schedule before modeling.)*

**The 2024 Child Care Cost Study** (DHR with Alabama State University; survey fielded **October 17 – November 14, 2023**; report dated **May 17, 2024**; 1,523 of 2,201 returned = 69%, 1,250 valid = 57%) documents the gap ([2024 Child Care Cost Study](https://dhr.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/2024-Child-Care-Cost-Study-Final-2024.05.17.pdf)):

**Day care center weekly rates, 75th percentile:**

| Region | Under 2½ yrs | 2½–5 yrs | School age |
|---|---|---|---|
| Region 3 — Birmingham | $225 | $204 | — |
| Region 1 — Huntsville | $210 | $195 | $170 |
| Region 8 — Talladega | $150 | — | — |
| Region 7 — Fort Payne | $145 | $135 | $126 |
| **Statewide** | **$205 (avg)** | **$195** | **$153** |

**Documented reimbursement gaps:**
- Huntsville, centers, under 2½: **$210 market vs. $168 Star-1 reimbursement = $42/week gap**
- Birmingham, centers, under 2½: **$220 market vs. $199 reimbursement = $21/week gap**
- The study concludes "almost all child care rates were higher than the reimbursement rate" and that "subsidy rates are below the 75th percentile" in most regions.

The draft plan separately reports a **statewide average reimbursement near $120/week** for full-time care, with **46% of providers charging above** it and an **average $24/week per-child shortfall**. ([FFY 2025–2027 CCDF Plan](https://dhr.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/CCDF-FFY-2025-2027-For-Alabama-2.pdf))

Quality STARS participants command higher private rates: **Star 2–5 centers averaged $190–$206 for infants versus $168 at Star 1.** ([2024 Cost Study](https://dhr.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/2024-Child-Care-Cost-Study-Final-2024.05.17.pdf))

**No rate increase has been announced for FY2026 or FY2027 — UNCONFIRMED whether one is in planning.** The 2024 cost study creates the evidentiary predicate for one; the FY2027 ETF gave DHR's child care line only **+$455,980** (see below), which is not enough to fund a meaningful across-the-board increase.

### Waitlist
Alabama's plan **does not describe a statewide waitlist**, operating instead on priority enrollment categories. ([2025–2027 CCDF State Plan](https://dhr.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/2025-2027-CCDF-State-Plan-with-Approval-Letter.pdf)) Historically the state eliminated its waitlist and advertised access "within one week of application," though that reporting dates to 2019. ([Alabama Arise](https://alarise.org/resources/enhanced-child-care-funding-makes-life-better-for-alabamas-children-and-families/) — **note: this Arise source is 2019-vintage; its funding and caseload figures should not be used as current**.)

### 2024 federal CCDF rule implementation
Outstanding items flagged in the approved plan ([2025–2027 CCDF State Plan](https://dhr.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/2025-2027-CCDF-State-Plan-with-Approval-Letter.pdf)):
1. **7% family copay cap** — waiver submitted, per-child methodology to be reformed
2. **12-month eligibility periods** and **reporting requirements during the eligibility period** — non-compliance notices received May 9, 2024
3. **Pediatric first aid pre-service training** (45 CFR 98.44) — non-compliant

### State appropriation
FY2027 ETF: **Child Care and Afterschool Program (DHR) = $91.97 million, up $455,980** over FY2026. ([A+ Education Partnership — Budget Watch: FY2027 ETF](https://aplusala.org/blog/2026/04/02/budget-watch-tracking-the-2027-education-trust-fund-budget/)) Effectively **flat**.

## 3. STATE PRE-K — Alabama First Class Pre-K

**This is the single largest growth opportunity in the state for a private operator.**

### Administration and scale
Administered by the **Alabama Department of Early Childhood Education (ADECE), Office of School Readiness**.

- **1,521 classrooms** currently, serving **24,168–24,238 four-year-olds** ([Alabama Reflector, June 25, 2026](https://alabamareflector.com/2026/06/25/alabama-to-expand-first-class-pre-k-program-to-64-classrooms-this-fall/); [NIEER 2025 Alabama profile](https://nieer.org/yearbook/2025/state-profiles/alabama))
- Classrooms in **every one of Alabama's 67 counties**
- **~40% of Alabama 4-year-olds** served in 2024-25 ([Alabama Daily News](https://aldailynews.com/alabama-adds-first-class-pre-k-classrooms-but-access-remains-an-issue/)); FY2027 budget projected to reach **47%** ([Alabama Reflector, March 13, 2026](https://alabamareflector.com/2026/03/13/alabama-house-approves-10-5-billion-educational-trust-fund-budget/))
- **20 consecutive years** as NIEER's highest-quality state pre-K — **10 of 10 quality benchmarks met**, unmatched nationally ([Gov. Ivey, June 2026](https://governor.alabama.gov/newsroom/2026/06/governor-ivey-announces-64-new-first-class-pre-k-classrooms-for-the-2026-27-school-year/); [NIEER](https://nieer.org/yearbook/2025/state-profiles/alabama))

### Funding
- **FY2027: $209.87 million** to the Office of School Readiness — **+$12.4 million over FY2026** (implying FY2026 ≈ **$197.5 million**) ([A+ Education Partnership](https://aplusala.org/blog/2026/04/02/budget-watch-tracking-the-2027-education-trust-fund-budget/))
- Alabama Reflector reported **"$226 million total pre-K funding"** (+~$13M) — this is the broader ADECE early-childhood total, not the School Readiness line alone ([Alabama Reflector](https://alabamareflector.com/2026/03/13/alabama-house-approves-10-5-billion-educational-trust-fund-budget/))
- Within FY2027: **$5.3 million to add 35 new classrooms**; **$4.4 million to maintain ~1,500 existing classrooms** (House-passed version)
- NIEER (2024-25): **$185,448,382** total state spending; **$7,651 state spending per child**; **$10,741 per child all funds**; local match required ([NIEER](https://nieer.org/yearbook/2025/state-profiles/alabama))

([ADECE — First Class Pre-K Funding](https://children.alabama.gov/for-educators/grants-funding/first-class-prek-funding/))

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| **New classroom setup** (one-time) | up to **$125,000** |
| **Tiered annual funding** | **$84,804 – $97,908** |
| **Excellence funding** | up to **$48,300/yr**, 3-year renewal cycle |
| **Per-pupil allocation** (non-Head Start) | up to **$3,600**, or $200 per enrolled student |

([NIEER](https://nieer.org/yearbook/2025/state-profiles/alabama))

### Mixed delivery — private/for-profit participation
First Class Pre-K explicitly funds classrooms in **"public schools, private child care centers, Head Start programs, community organizations, faith-based centers, colleges and universities, and military agencies."** ([NIEER](https://nieer.org/yearbook/2025/state-profiles/alabama); [ADECE funding page](https://children.alabama.gov/for-educators/grants-funding/first-class-prek-funding/); [Alabama School Readiness Alliance](https://alabamaschoolreadiness.org/about-first-class-pre-k/))

**The precise count of First Class Pre-K classrooms located in private child care centers is NOT published in any source I could reach — UNCONFIRMED.** Neither the NIEER state profile, the ADECE site, the Governor's releases, nor the Alabama School Readiness Alliance breaks out classroom counts by auspice. This is the **highest-value open data item** in this report. Obtain it directly from ADECE Office of School Readiness (334-224-3171) or via the annual First Class Pre-K grantee list, which names each site.

### Program requirements a private grantee must meet
([NIEER](https://nieer.org/yearbook/2025/state-profiles/alabama); [ADECE](https://www.children.alabama.gov/adece/first-class-pre-k/))
- **Maximum class size 20**; **staff-child ratio 1:10 or better** — materially tighter than the 1:18 licensing ratio for 4-year-olds
- **Lead teacher must hold a bachelor's degree** (pre-K specialization preferred); assistant teacher CDA or equivalent
- **Minimum 15 hours** annual professional development
- **6.5-hour** standard instructional day
- Universal eligibility: children age 4 by September 1, Alabama residents; **no income test**; **no registration fee to families**
- Screening/referral services and continuous quality improvement with structured observation required

**This is the core trade-off:** a First Class Pre-K classroom brings **$85K–$98K annually plus up to $125K in setup**, but imposes BA-credentialed lead teachers and 1:10 ratios on that classroom — a substantially higher cost structure than a standard Alabama 4-year-old room.

### Expansion runway
- **64 new classrooms announced June 24-25, 2026** for the 2026-27 school year, serving ~1,100–1,152 additional children ([Gov. Ivey](https://governor.alabama.gov/newsroom/2026/06/governor-ivey-announces-64-new-first-class-pre-k-classrooms-for-the-2026-27-school-year/); [Alabama Reflector](https://alabamareflector.com/2026/06/25/alabama-to-expand-first-class-pre-k-program-to-64-classrooms-this-fall/))
- **~4,100 children on waiting lists in 20 priority counties** identified by a University of Alabama study; clearing them would require **229 additional classrooms** ([Alabama Daily News](https://aldailynews.com/alabama-adds-first-class-pre-k-classrooms-but-access-remains-an-issue/))
- Named expansion barriers: **available classroom space, transportation, before/after-school care, and workforce capacity** — every one of which an established multi-site operator with existing licensed square footage is positioned to solve
- NIEER recommends Alabama move toward universal pre-K (≥70% of eligible children) ([Alabama Reflector](https://alabamareflector.com/2026/06/25/alabama-to-expand-first-class-pre-k-program-to-64-classrooms-this-fall/))

### Federal overlay
Alabama received a **$3.8 million Preschool Development Grant Birth through Five (PDG B-5)** from ACF, awarded December 2025 and announced **January 21, 2026**. Administered by ADECE, it funds workforce supply/stability/skills, **alignment across mixed-delivery programs**, family engagement, and cross-agency analytics — with emphasis on rural and underserved communities. ([Gov. Ivey, January 2026](https://governor.alabama.gov/newsroom/2026/01/governor-ivey-announces-3-8-million-grant-to-further-strengthen-early-childhood-education-system-in-alabama/))

## 4. QUALITY RATING SYSTEM — Alabama Quality STARS

### Structure and administration
**ADECE administers Quality STARS** under an interagency agreement with DHR. ([Alabama Quality STARS FAQs](https://alabamaqualitystars.org/faqs/))

- **Five levels**, "building block" design — all requirements at each level must be met before advancing
- Standards assess: staff qualifications and professional development; management and administrative practices; learning environment and curriculum; family involvement and community partnerships ([Alabama Childcare Facts](https://alabamachildcarefacts.com/alabama-quality-stars/))
- **STAR 1 is automatic**: "Licensed centers and homes meet the criteria for a one (1) star rating… There are no additional requirements for a STAR 1 Rating" — **unless the program opts out**
- Programs may apply for a higher rating **after one year of licensure**; STAR 2+ programs may apply for a higher-rating assessment **once per year**
- **STAR 2 and above are valid for 36 months** and must be renewed before expiration
- Only **licensed centers** are rated; unlicensable programs (military, public school, Alabama Community College System) may participate
([Alabama Quality STARS FAQs](https://alabamaqualitystars.org/faqs/))

### Financial ties — three of them
1. **Subsidy tiering:** +2% / +4% / +6% / +8% / +10% above base rate for STAR 1–5 ([DHR](https://dhr.alabama.gov/child-care-subsidies-rates-paid-by-the-department-of-human-resources-to-increase/))
2. **Annual incentive payment** — a one-time annual payment from DHR, effective October 1, 2021; STAR 1 programs that opted out receive nothing ([Quality STARS Incentive Payment Matrix](https://alabamaqualitystars.org/alabama-quality-stars-bonus-incentive-schedules/)) — *specific dollar amounts by star level are behind the matrix documents and are* **UNCONFIRMED**
3. **Tax credit gateway** — participation in Quality STARS is a **hard eligibility requirement** for BOTH the employer credit and the facility credit (§5 below), and the facility credit scales directly with star level

### Status
The system is operational statewide and is the linchpin of Alabama's incentive architecture. **Current participation counts by star level are not published — UNCONFIRMED.** The 2024 cost study offers indirect evidence of meaningful uptake, since it was able to compare Star 2–5 versus Star 1 pricing at statistically useful sample sizes. ([2024 Cost Study](https://dhr.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/2024-Child-Care-Cost-Study-Final-2024.05.17.pdf))

**Operator action item:** any Alabama center not rated at STAR 4 or 5 is leaving money on the table across all three channels simultaneously. The delta between STAR 1 ($1,000/child) and STAR 5 ($2,000/child) on the facility credit alone is worth up to the full $25,000 facility cap.

## 5. 2025–2026 LEGISLATION

### The Child Care Tax Credit Act — HB 358 (2024)

Signed by Governor Ivey **May 9, 2024**; **effective January 1, 2025**; codified at **Ala. Code Title 40, Ch. 18, Article 28 — "Child Care and Workforce Development"** (see [§ 40-18-545](https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/title-40/chapter-18/article-28/section-40-18-545/) on rulemaking, assignment of credits, refunds, and the Child Care Tax Credit Account). ([VOICES for Alabama's Children](https://alavoices.org/issues/child_care_tax_credit/))

*Sponsor: widely attributed to House Minority Leader Rep. Anthony Daniels (D-Huntsville) — I was unable to verify the sponsor line directly in this pass; treat as* **UNCONFIRMED**. *The bill moved through House committee April 16, 2024 and Senate committee May 2, 2024 ([Alabama Reflector](https://alabamareflector.com/2024/04/16/alabama-house-committee-passes-child-care-tax-credit-bill/); [Alabama Reflector](https://alabamareflector.com/2024/05/02/alabama-senate-committee-approves-tax-breaks-for-child-care-housing/)).*

**Total package value: $82.5 million over three years if fully utilized** ([VOICES for Alabama's Children](https://alavoices.org/)).

#### (a) Employer Childcare Tax Credit
([Alabama Dept. of Revenue — Employer Tax Credit](https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/tax-incentives/employer-tax-credit/); [BMSS](https://www.bmss.com/alabama-childcare-tax-credits-2025-2027-opportunities-for-employers-and-childcare-providers/))

- **<25 employees: 100%** of eligible expenses; **25+ employees: 75%**
- **Cap: $600,000 per employer per year**
- Eligible employees: Alabama residents, full- or part-time, including independent contractors, with **wages ≤ $80,000** (excluding overtime/bonuses); children **aged 5 or under**
- **Provider must be DHR-licensed AND participating in Alabama Quality STARS**, or operated by a public higher education institution
- **Statewide aggregate caps: $15M (2025) → $17.5M (2026) → $20M (2027)**
- **25% reserved** for small businesses or employers headquartered in targeted rural counties
- Non-refundable; **no carryforward**
- Reservations via **My Alabama Taxes**, first-come first-served; **opened January 5, 2026** for TY2026 (March 1, 2025 for TY2025)

#### (b) Childcare Facility Tax Credit — **for-profit providers only**
([Alabama Dept. of Revenue — Childcare Facility Tax Credit](https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/tax-incentives/childcare-facility-tax-credit/); [procedures](https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/tax-incentives/procedures-for-childcare-facility-tax-credit/))

Credit per average monthly eligible **subsidy-enrolled child aged 5 and under**, scaled by Quality STARS rating:

| STARS rating | Credit per child |
|---|---|
| 5-star | **$2,000** |
| 4-star | **$1,750** |
| 3-star | **$1,500** |
| 2-star | **$1,250** |
| 1-star | **$1,000** |

- **Cap: $25,000 per qualifying facility per year**
- **Statewide aggregate cap: $5 million per year**, with **25% set aside for rural-area facilities**; unused rural allocation moves to the general queue **after July 1**
- Eligibility: **for-profit entity**, current DHR licensure, Quality STARS participation, enrolls subsidy children ≤5
- **Refundable**; non-transferable except to pass-through entity owners pro rata; **no carryforward**
- Claim **no later than March 1** following the tax year

**This is the headline provision for the client.** Most state child care credits exclude for-profit operators; Alabama's is written for them. A multi-site operator with, say, 15 Alabama centers at STAR 4–5 with meaningful subsidy census could plausibly capture **up to $375,000/year** — but only against a **$5M statewide pool on first-come, first-served reservation**. Reservation timing is the whole game.

## 8. BUSINESS-CLIMATE ISSUES FOR A FOR-PROFIT OPERATOR

### Workforce and wages
The binding constraint statewide. Named by advocates as one of four barriers to pre-K expansion alongside classroom space, transportation, and wraparound care. ([Alabama Daily News](https://aldailynews.com/alabama-adds-first-class-pre-k-classrooms-but-access-remains-an-issue/))

Structural features:
- Licensing floor for teachers is **HS diploma + 12 hours/year** — a low credential bar that keeps entry wages low but caps quality advancement
- **First Class Pre-K requires a BA lead teacher** — creating a two-tier internal labor market inside any center that hosts an FCPK classroom, and direct wage competition with public school systems
- Alabama's 2025 **parental leave law for teachers and state employees** (8 weeks/2 weeks full pay) raises the benchmark employees compare private employers against
- **T.E.A.C.H.** (via Alabama Partnership for Children) is the primary credential-subsidy vehicle — under-leveraged by most operators
- The **PDG B-5 grant explicitly funds "increasing supply, stability, and skills of early childhood professionals"** ([Gov. Ivey](https://governor.alabama.gov/newsroom/2026/01/governor-ivey-announces-3-8-million-grant-to-further-strengthen-early-childhood-education-system-in-alabama/)) — an access point worth pursuing with ADECE

*Alabama-specific current wage data for child care workers was not obtainable within budget — recommend pulling BLS OES 39-9011 (Childcare Workers) and 25-2011 (Preschool Teachers) for Alabama MSAs directly.* **UNCONFIRMED.**

### Tax credit eligibility — the standout advantage
- The **Childcare Facility Tax Credit is restricted to for-profit entities** — an unusual and directly favorable design ([Alabama Dept. of Revenue](https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/tax-incentives/childcare-facility-tax-credit/))
- **Refundable** (unlike the employer credit), so it converts to cash even against low Alabama tax liability
- But: **$25,000/facility/year cap** and a **$5M statewide pool**, first-come first-served, reservation window opening early January
- **Requires both** DHR licensure **and** Quality STARS participation, and scales with star level — making STARS advancement a direct, quantifiable ROI calculation
- The client is likely eligible **on both sides**: as a **provider** (facility credit) and as an **employer** claiming the employer credit for its own staff's child care, since its own centers are licensed and STARS-rated. Confirm related-party treatment with Alabama counsel.
- Rural set-aside (25%, released to general queue after July 1) creates a **timing arbitrage** for non-rural centers: reserve early in January for the general pool, and monitor July 1 for rural spillover.

### Zoning and siting
**Purely local in Alabama — no state preemption for child care siting was found.** Municipal zoning codes govern. Fire and health department inspection reports with **no violations** are prerequisites for licensure ([Alabama Childcare Facts](https://alabamachildcarefacts.com/minimum-standards/)), so local fire marshal and county health department relationships are gating items for any new site. **State-level zoning preemption legislation: none identified in 2025 or 2026.**

### Liability and insurance
- No state-mandated minimum liability coverage for **licensed** centers surfaced in the accessible materials — **UNCONFIRMED**; the requirement, if any, lives in 660-5-26's administration rules
- Notably, **license-exempt religious facilities are explicitly required to show proof of property, casualty, and liability coverage** under the Child Care Safety Act ([DHR](https://dhr.alabama.gov/child-care/license-exempt-day-care-facilities/)) — an asymmetry worth confirming, since it implies licensed centers' requirements sit in the licensing rules rather than statute
- Alabama retains **contributory negligence** (one of only a handful of states), which is broadly favorable to defendants in premises and supervision claims
- Transportation carries **distinct ratio requirements**; vehicle operations are a discrete risk and rule category under 660-5-26

### Competitive structure
The **religious exemption is the defining feature of Alabama's competitive landscape.** Exempt church-based centers operate without licensing, without ratio enforcement, and without staff qualification mandates, at a materially lower cost structure. They are excluded from Quality STARS, and therefore from both tax credits and subsidy tiering. **A licensed for-profit operator's strategy should lean hard into the incentive stack the exempt sector cannot touch** — STARS 4/5 rating, facility tax credit, subsidy tiering, and First Class Pre-K grants — rather than competing on private-pay price.
